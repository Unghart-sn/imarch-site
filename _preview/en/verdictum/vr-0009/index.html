PREAMBLE

Operational Governance defines the governance system of Chronicle OS as an international institutional infrastructure for scholarly communication.

This Volume establishes the principles governing the distribution of authority, responsibility and managerial decision-making. These principles ensure the sustainable operation of Chronicle OS irrespective of the scale of the system or the number of participants.

All documents of this Volume form an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and are applied in conjunction with Volume I (Institutional Codex) and Volume II (Institutional Relations Package).

 

GOV-001 — CHRONICLE OS GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

Document ID: GOV-001

Document Type: Governance Framework

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the governance system of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system of scholarly communication.

The document establishes the principles governing the distribution of authority, responsibility and managerial decision-making. These principles ensure the sustainable operation of Chronicle OS irrespective of the scale of the system or the number of participants.

Chapter 2. Governance Objectives

The Chronicle OS governance system ensures:

  • institutional resilience;
  • coherence of action among all participants;
  • preservation of the autonomy of University Chronicle Nodes;
  • compliance with the normative framework;
  • continuous development of the international ecosystem;
  • protection of the long-term interests of the scholarly community.

Chapter 3. Fundamental Governance Principles

Chronicle OS governance is founded upon the following principles.

3.1. Institutional Primacy

The interests of the institution take precedence. The interests of individual officials, units or organisations do not take precedence over the interests of the institution.

3.2. Distributed Agency

Each University Chronicle Node retains its own institutional autonomy.

Chronicle OS coordinates interaction. Chronicle OS does not replace the internal governance of the university.

3.3. Delegation of Responsibility

Authority is delegated to the level at which the relevant decision may be taken most professionally and efficiently.

3.4. Normative Governance

Every managerial decision is grounded in the applicable normative documentation of Chronicle OS.

Personal preferences, traditions or situational circumstances do not replace established procedures.

3.5. Architectural Consistency

The development of individual Chronicle OS components does not compromise the integrity of the entire institutional architecture.

Every change is assessed from the standpoint of its impact upon the system as a whole.

3.6. Transparency of Accountability

Every governance function has a designated bearer of responsibility.

No process may exist without an established level of accountability.

3.7. Evolutionary Development

Chronicle OS is regarded as a continuously evolving institutional system.

Procedures are improved through the progressive refinement of the normative framework. Such refinement does not destroy the previously established architecture.

Chapter 4. Governance Levels of Chronicle OS

The Chronicle OS governance system is organised as a distributed multi-level architecture.

Each level possesses its own competence. No level substitutes the functions of another.

Governance is built upon the principle of distributed responsibility. The unity of the normative framework is preserved.

The following governance levels are distinguished within Chronicle OS:

  • the International Level;
  • the Institutional Level;
  • the Local Level;
  • the Operational Level.

Each level operates within the limits of its own competence. The levels interact through approved protocols.

Chapter 5. International Governance Level

The International Level ensures the strategic development of Chronicle OS.

Its competence includes:

  • development of the institutional architecture;
  • approval of normative documentation;
  • formulation of international policy;
  • development of international partnership;
  • coordination of long-term programmes;
  • ensuring the unity of the normative environment;
  • development of the Chronicle Registry;
  • maintaining the integrity of the Documentation System.

The International Level does not exercise operational governance over universities.

Its task is to maintain the resilience of the entire international ecosystem.

Chapter 6. Institutional Governance Level

The Institutional Level is represented by the University Chronicle Node.

Each University Chronicle Node independently organises:

  • editorial activity;
  • internal procedures;
  • interaction with authors;
  • interaction with experts;
  • organisation of the publication process;
  • local digital infrastructure;
  • development of university scholarly communication.

The Institutional Level adopts decisions independently. Such decisions do not contravene the normative framework of Chronicle OS.

Chronicle OS does not interfere in the academic autonomy of the university, provided that the activity of the University Chronicle Node complies with the applicable normative documentation.

Each University Chronicle Node bears responsibility for the quality of its own institutional activity. The Node simultaneously participates in the formation of the shared space of international scholarly trust.

Chapter 7. Operational Governance Level

The Operational Level ensures the direct execution of the day-to-day processes of Chronicle OS.

The Operational Level comprises:

  • the Editor-in-Chief;
  • the Managing Editor;
  • the Editorial Secretary;
  • the Expert Network Coordinator;
  • the Chronicle Registry Operator;
  • the Technical Editor;
  • the System Administrator of the University Chronicle Node;
  • other officials determined by the internal structure of the university.

The Operational Level implements approved procedures. The Operational Level does not modify the normative architecture of the system.

Chapter 8. Distribution of Authority

Authority is distributed according to the principle of functional competence.

Strategic matters are decided at the International Level.

Institutional matters are decided by the University Chronicle Node.

Operational matters are decided by the officials responsible for the relevant processes.

Delegation of authority is permitted within the limits of the established normative framework.

The concentration of incompatible functions in a single participant is not permitted where this may affect the objectivity of decisions taken.

Chapter 9. Managerial Decision-Making

All managerial decisions of Chronicle OS are classified into the following categories:

Strategic decisions — determine the long-term development of the international system.

Institutional decisions — relate to the organisation of the activity of the University Chronicle Node.

Operational decisions — ensure the execution of daily working processes.

Architectural decisions — relate to changes in the Chronicle OS normative framework, the development of the Documentation System, the Chronicle Registry and other foundational components of the system.

Architectural decisions are adopted following an analysis of their impact upon the integrity of the entire institutional model.

Every decision:

  • is documented;
  • is traceable;
  • is reproducible;
  • complies with the applicable Chronicle OS normative framework.

Chapter 10. Architectural Oversight

Architectural oversight ensures the preservation of the integrity of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system.

The subject of architectural oversight comprises:

  • normative documentation;
  • organisational structure;
  • digital infrastructure;
  • institutional procedures;
  • mechanisms of interaction among participants;
  • development of the Documentation System.

Architectural oversight does not replace operational governance.

Its task is to ensure that changes correspond to the general architecture of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 11. Change Management

Chronicle OS regards change as a natural part of institutional development.

Every change complies with the following requirements:

  • it has a documented justification;
  • it takes account of its impact upon the existing normative framework;
  • it preserves compatibility with previously adopted decisions;
  • it ensures traceability of changes;
  • it does not compromise the institutional resilience of the system.

Changes to normative documentation are effected through the official versioning of the Documentation System.

Each edition becomes part of the institutional memory of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 12. Final Provisions

The Framework is applied in conjunction with:

  • the Chronicle OS Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
  • the Documentation System;
  • the Institutional Relations Package (see Volume II);
  • Operational Procedures;
  • Chronicle Registry Standards (see Volume I);
  • the Certification Framework (see Volume II, INS-007).

The document defines the foundational governance model of Chronicle OS. The document is the basis for the development of operational procedures, working regulations and digital scenarios for the functioning of the international institutional ecosystem.

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-002 — INTERNATIONAL GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

Document ID: GOV-002

Document Type: International Governance Structure

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the international organisational structure of Chronicle OS. The document establishes the system for the distribution of functions among international governance bodies and determines their competence.

Chapter 2. Principle of Distributed Governance

Chronicle OS does not employ a centralised administrative model.

International governance is exercised through the interaction of specialised bodies. Each body is responsible for its own area of competence.

No governance body possesses absolute authority.

The system is built upon the principle of mutual institutional balance.

Chapter 3. International Institutional Level

The highest level of international governance ensures:

  • preservation of the institutional architecture of Chronicle OS;
  • development of the normative framework;
  • international coordination;
  • protection of the long-term interests of the international ecosystem.

This level does not exercise operational governance over universities and does not interfere in their academic activity.

Chapter 4. International Architecture Council

The International Architecture Council undertakes:

  • development of the Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
  • development of the Documentation System;
  • approval of new normative documents;
  • consideration of architectural changes;
  • oversight of the compatibility of new decisions with the existing institutional model.

Decisions of the Council are adopted on the basis of the normative documentation of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 5. International Editorial Council

The International Editorial Council ensures:

  • development of international editorial policy;
  • coordination of the activity of editorial boards;
  • development of Publication Ethics (see Volume I, DOC-004);
  • support of international scholarly programmes;
  • development of the quality of scholarly communication.

The Editorial Council does not interfere in the independent peer review of individual publications.

Chapter 6. International Expert Council

The International Expert Council is responsible for:

  • development of the Chronicle OS expert network;
  • formation of criteria for expert activity;
  • development of reviewer training programmes;
  • evaluation of the professional standards of the expert community.

Chapter 7. International Digital Infrastructure Council

The Digital Infrastructure Council is responsible for the development of the technological environment of Chronicle OS.

Its competence comprises:

  • development of the Chronicle Registry;
  • development of the Chronicle Node;
  • digital identification of participants;
  • integration of international services;
  • ensuring the compatibility of digital components;
  • support of the Documentation System in the digital environment;
  • development of tools for the automation of scholarly communication.

The Council ensures the technological resilience of the international ecosystem.

Chapter 8. International Certification Council

The Certification Council undertakes:

  • development of criteria for institutional evaluation;
  • development of Chronicle Certification standards;
  • support of audit procedures;
  • confirmation of the compliance of University Chronicle Nodes with the requirements of Chronicle OS;
  • improvement of institutional maturity models.

The Council acts independently of editorial bodies.

Chapter 9. Coordination of Council Activities

International Councils interact on the basis of the approved normative documentation.

Decisions are adopted within the limits of their own competence.

Where matters affecting several areas of activity are considered, temporary joint working groups are formed.

No Council may alter the competence of another Council.

Chapter 10. Principle of Collegiality

Strategic decisions are adopted collegially.

In the discussion, account is taken of:

  • scholarly consequences;
  • institutional consequences;
  • architectural consequences;
  • technological consequences;
  • international consequences.

Collegiality is regarded as a mechanism for ensuring the resilience of the decisions adopted.

Chapter 11. Principle of Institutional Independence

Each international governance body exercises its activity independently of commercial, political and other external interests.

The principal criteria for decision-making are:

  • scholarly integrity;
  • institutional resilience;
  • compliance with the Documentation System;
  • compliance with the Institutional Codex (see Volume I).

Chapter 12. Final Provisions

The document is applied in conjunction with:

  • the Chronicle OS Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
  • the Documentation System;
  • the Governance Framework (GOV-001);
  • the Institutional Relations Package (see Volume II);
  • Chronicle Registry Standards (see Volume I).

The structure of international governance of Chronicle OS may develop as the international network grows. Such development is carried out without violating the principles of the distributed institutional architecture.

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-003 — INSTITUTIONAL ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

Document ID: GOV-003

Document Type: Institutional Roles and Responsibilities

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the principal institutional roles of participants in Chronicle OS. The document establishes the limits of their competence and distributes responsibility among the levels of governance of the international ecosystem.

The document is the normative basis for the subsequent development of operational procedures and working regulations.

Chapter 2. Principle of Functional Role

In Chronicle OS, responsibility is determined not by position, but by the function performed.

A single natural person may perform several roles, provided that this does not violate the principles of independence, objectivity and separation of authority.

The same role may be performed by different persons in different University Chronicle Nodes.

Chapter 3. System Architect

The System Architect is responsible for the development of the institutional model of Chronicle OS.

Their competence includes:

  • development of the architecture of the Documentation System;
  • harmonisation of the normative framework;
  • ensuring the compatibility of new components;
  • development of the long-term institutional model;
  • analysis of systemic changes.

The System Architect does not exercise operational governance over editorial activity.

Chapter 4. Institutional Coordinator

The Institutional Coordinator ensures interaction between the University Chronicle Node and the international Chronicle OS ecosystem.

Their duties include:

  • support of the university’s connection;
  • coordination of the exchange of information;
  • support of institutional interaction;
  • consultation on matters of the normative framework;
  • assistance in the development of the University Chronicle Node.

Chapter 5. Editor-in-Chief of the University Chronicle Node

The Editor-in-Chief exercises scholarly leadership over the editorial activity of the University Chronicle Node.

Their competence includes:

  • formation of editorial policy;
  • ensuring the scholarly quality of publications;
  • interaction with the editorial board;
  • adoption of final editorial decisions;
  • representation of the University Chronicle Node in the international ecosystem.

The Editor-in-Chief is responsible for compliance with Publication Ethics (see Volume I, DOC-004) and for scholarly integrity.

Chapter 6. Managing Editor

The Managing Editor ensures the organisation of the editorial process of the University Chronicle Node.

Their duties include:

  • coordination of the work of the editorial office;
  • distribution of manuscripts;
  • oversight of procedural compliance;
  • interaction with reviewers;
  • preparation of materials for publication.

The Managing Editor does not adopt final decisions on publication unless otherwise provided by the internal regulations of the University Chronicle Node.

Chapter 7. Chronicle Registry Operator

The Chronicle Registry Operator ensures the maintenance of the institutional registry of Chronicle OS.

Their competence includes:

  • registration of institutional objects;
  • maintenance of the register of publications;
  • support of registration records;
  • oversight of the integrity of metadata;
  • ensuring the traceability of institutional history;
  • archiving of registration information.

The Registry Operator does not modify the scholarly content of publications and does not adopt editorial decisions.

Chapter 8. Technical Editor

The Technical Editor is responsible for the preparation of materials for publication.

Their responsibilities include:

  • verification of the formatting of materials;
  • oversight of the completeness of metadata;
  • compliance with the requirements of the Documentation System;
  • preparation of digital publication files;
  • interaction with the digital infrastructure of Chronicle OS.

The Technical Editor does not perform scholarly peer review of manuscripts.

Chapter 9. System Administrator of the University Chronicle Node

The System Administrator ensures the operability of the local digital infrastructure of the University Chronicle Node.

They are responsible for:

  • the functioning of digital services;
  • the management of access rights;
  • the support of local components of the Chronicle Node;
  • information security;
  • technical support for users.

Chapter 10. Principle of Separation of Functions

Chronicle OS is built upon the mandatory separation of functions.

The combination, in a single role, of authority capable of affecting the objectivity of scholarly evaluation is not permitted.

Editorial activity, expert evaluation, registration and technical support are regarded as autonomous functional domains.

Chapter 11. Personal Accountability

Each participant in Chronicle OS bears personal responsibility for the execution of the functions assigned to their institutional role.

Responsibility is determined by:

  • the scope of authority;
  • the nature of the functions performed;
  • the applicable normative documentation;
  • the institutional obligations assumed.

Chapter 12. Final Provisions

The document is applied in conjunction with:

  • the Governance Framework (GOV-001);
  • the International Governance Structure (GOV-002);
  • the Institutional Relations Package (see Volume II);
  • Operational Procedures;
  • Chronicle Registry Standards (see Volume I).

The catalogue of institutional roles may be expanded as Chronicle OS develops. Such expansion is carried out without altering the foundational principles governing the distribution of responsibility.

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-004 — DECISION MAKING FRAMEWORK

Document ID: GOV-004

Document Type: Decision Making Framework

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document establishes the principles governing the preparation, consideration, adoption, recording and execution of managerial decisions within the international institutional ecosystem of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 2. Principle of Normative Decision-Making

In Chronicle OS, every managerial decision is adopted not on the basis of personal discretion, but on the basis of the applicable normative documentation.

Where no corresponding normative basis exists, a decision is taken to develop a new normative document or to amend the existing documentation.

Chapter 3. Classification of Decisions

All Chronicle OS decisions are classified into the following categories.

Architectural — alter the constitution of Chronicle OS.

Normative — alter the Documentation System.

Institutional — relate to the activity of the University Chronicle Node.

Operational — ensure the execution of daily activity.

Technical — relate to the functioning of the digital infrastructure.

Chapter 4. Preparation of a Decision

Every decision passes through the following stages:

  • initiation;
  • analysis of the normative framework;
  • assessment of institutional consequences;
  • consultation;
  • approval;
  • registration;
  • execution;
  • archiving.

The omission of individual stages without a normative basis is not permitted.

Chapter 5. Principle of Traceability

Every decision adopted is capable of subsequent reconstruction.

For each decision, the following are recorded:

  • the initiator;
  • the date;
  • the normative basis;
  • the level of adoption;
  • the responsible executors;
  • the status of execution;
  • the connection with other decisions.

Chapter 6. Registration of Decisions

All strategic and normative decisions are subject to registration in the Chronicle Registry.

Registration ensures:

  • institutional memory;
  • legal traceability;
  • the possibility of subsequent analysis;
  • the preservation of the history of the development of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-005 — PRINCIPLES OF INSTITUTIONAL OPERATION

Document ID: GOV-005

Document Type: Principles of Institutional Operation

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the foundational principles of the functioning of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system of scholarly communication.

The principles set forth in this document are binding upon all participants in Chronicle OS irrespective of their level of authority and institutional affiliation.

Chapter 2. Principle of Institutional Trust

Trust is regarded as the principal strategic resource of Chronicle OS.

Every element of the system contributes to the strengthening of international trust through compliance with normative requirements, transparency of activity and the reproducibility of decisions adopted.

Chapter 3. Principle of Scholarly Autonomy

Each University Chronicle Node independently determines the directions of its own scholarly activity.

Chronicle OS provides unified mechanisms of scholarly communication. Chronicle OS does not interfere in the content of academic research.

Chapter 4. Principle of Normative Continuity

All processes of Chronicle OS have a normative description.

No critically important process may be based on oral agreements, personal traditions or the individual experience of particular participants.

Every reproducible activity is subject to documentation.

Chapter 5. Principle of Digital Traceability

Every institutional process is capable of digital support.

Digital recording ensures:

  • traceability of decisions;
  • preservation of institutional memory;
  • the possibility of subsequent analysis;
  • reproducibility of procedures;
  • protection against the loss of critically important information.

Chapter 6. Principle of Minimal Administrative Burden

Chronicle OS strives to reduce administrative procedures that do not affect the quality of scholarly communication.

Automation of processes is regarded as the principal instrument for reducing the operational burden on university editorial offices.

Normative documentation describes processes in such a manner that the greatest possible proportion of them may be implemented by means of digital infrastructure.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-006 — INSTITUTIONAL CONTINUITY FRAMEWORK

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Edition: Master Edition

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the mechanisms ensuring the continuous functioning of Chronicle OS. Such functioning is ensured irrespective of changes in the composition of participants, organisational structure, digital infrastructure or external conditions.

Institutional continuity is regarded as one of the foundational principles of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 2. Principle of Independence from Individuals

Chronicle OS operates independently of specific natural persons.

The replacement of participants in the system does not entail any change to the architecture of processes, the normative framework or the principles governing the operation of the international ecosystem.

No institutional function depends upon the competence of a single individual.

Chapter 3. Principle of Preservation of Institutional Memory

All significant decisions, normative documents, versions, registration records and architectural changes are subject to preservation within the Chronicle Registry.

Institutional memory is regarded as an autonomous asset of Chronicle OS. Institutional memory ensures the continuity of the system’s development.

Chapter 4. Principle of Reproducibility

Every process described by the normative documentation of Chronicle OS is reproducible irrespective of the individual performing it.

The transfer of functions between participants is effected without altering the logic of the processes.

Reproducibility is achieved through:

  • the Documentation System;
  • Operational Procedures;
  • the digital scenarios of Chronicle OS;
  • the Chronicle Registry.

Chapter 5. Principle of Scalability

The architecture of Chronicle OS permits the progressive expansion of the international network without altering the foundational governance model.

The connection of new University Chronicle Nodes is effected through the applicable normative procedures.

The development of the network does not require any change to the foundational principles of the Institutional Codex (see Volume I).

Chapter 6. Principle of Sustainable Development

All changes to Chronicle OS are assessed from the standpoint of their long-term impact upon the international institutional system.

Priority is given to decisions that ensure:

  • resilience;
  • compatibility;
  • normative integrity;
  • the development of scholarly communication;
  • the preservation of the trust of the international academic community.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

 

GOV-007 — CONTEXT GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

Document ID: GOV-007

Document Type: Context Governance Framework

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the principles governing the management of the institutional context of Chronicle OS.

The institutional context is understood as the aggregate of normative, organisational, digital and scholarly interconnections. These interconnections determine the state of the international ecosystem of scholarly communication.

Chapter 2. Context as an Object of Governance

In Chronicle OS, the object of governance is neither an individual publication nor an individual participant in the system.

The object of governance is the institutional context within which scholarly communication is carried out.

A change of context is regarded as a change in the state of the entire system.

Chapter 3. Formation of Context

The institutional context is formed through the interaction of the following components:

  • the Documentation System;
  • the Chronicle Registry;
  • University Chronicle Nodes;
  • the expert network;
  • the editorial network;
  • the digital infrastructure;
  • the normative framework;
  • institutional memory.

No single component forms the context on its own.

The context arises as the result of their joint functioning.

Chapter 4. Maintenance of Context

The maintenance of the institutional context is effected through:

  • compliance with the Documentation System;
  • the updating of the normative framework;
  • the preservation of institutional memory;
  • the registration of changes;
  • the support of the digital infrastructure;
  • the development of international cooperation.

Chapter 5. Change of Context

Any change to the institutional context is:

  • normatively justified;
  • documented;
  • registered;
  • assessed from the standpoint of its impact upon the international system.

A change of the local context of a University Chronicle Node does not compromise the integrity of the international context of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 6. Contextual Resilience

Chronicle OS regards the resilience of the institutional context as one of the most important indicators of the maturity of the system.

The context is considered resilient where changes to individual components do not lead to the destruction of the general architecture of scholarly communication.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the principles governing the evolutionary development of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system of scholarly communication.

Chapter 2. Principle of Continuous Development

Chronicle OS is regarded as an open institutional system.

Development is its natural state.

The absence of development is regarded as a factor of institutional risk.

Chapter 3. Sources of Development

The basis for the development of Chronicle OS may comprise:

  • changes in the international scholarly environment;
  • the development of digital technologies;

Each University Chronicle Node is a source of institutional feedback.

Practical operational experience is regarded as one of the principal mechanisms for the improvement of Chronicle OS.

The information obtained is used for the preparation of new editions of normative documentation.

Chapter 5. Institutional Experiments

Chronicle OS permits the conduct of limited institutional experiments.

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the principles governing the formation, competence and procedure of activity of the institutional committees of Chronicle OS.

Committees are specialised collegiate bodies. Committees are established to consider matters requiring interdisciplinary, expert or normative assessment.

Chapter 2. Principle of the Committee Model

Chronicle OS employs the committee model of governance as a mechanism of collective professional expertise.

A committee does not replace the activity of individual officials. A committee ensures the consideration of matters that extend beyond the competence of a single institutional role.

Chapter 3. Standing Committees

The following standing committees may operate within Chronicle OS:

  • the Architectural Committee;
  • the Publication Ethics Committee;
  • the Digital Infrastructure Committee;
  • the Certification Committee;
  • the Institutional Development Committee;
  • the International Cooperation Committee.

Where necessary, the list of committees may be expanded.

Chapter 4. Ad Hoc Committees

Temporary committees may be established to address special tasks.

The basis for their establishment comprises:

  • the implementation of an international project;
  • the conduct of an institutional audit;
  • the development of a new normative document;
  • the resolution of complex disputed situations;
  • the conduct of an institutional experiment.

Upon completion of the assigned task, the temporary committee ceases its activity.

Chapter 5. Competence of Committees

A committee is entitled:

  • to consider matters within the limits of its own competence;
  • to prepare recommendations;
  • to initiate the development of normative documents;
  • to submit proposals to international councils;
  • to conduct expert discussions;
  • to form working groups.

A committee is not entitled independently to alter the applicable normative documentation of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 6. Principle of Committee Independence

Committees exercise their activity independently of administrative pressure, commercial interests and external influence.

The consideration of matters is carried out on the basis of:

  • the Documentation System;
  • the Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
  • the applicable Frameworks;
  • registered materials;
  • the principles of scholarly integrity.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

GOV-010 — INSTITUTIONAL CONFLICT RESOLUTION FRAMEWORK

Document ID: GOV-010

Document Type: Institutional Conflict Resolution Framework

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the procedure for the consideration of institutional conflicts arising in the activity of Chronicle OS.

An institutional conflict is understood as any contradiction between participants, governance bodies or University Chronicle Nodes. A conflict is capable of affecting the resilience of the international system of scholarly communication.

Chapter 2. Fundamental Principles of Conflict Resolution

The resolution of conflicts is carried out on the basis of the following principles:

  • institutional impartiality;
  • normative justification;
  • equality of participants;
  • documentation of procedures;
  • the priority of preserving international trust.

Personal relations between participants are not regarded as a basis for the adoption of decisions.

Chapter 3. Categories of Conflicts

Conflicts are classified into:

  • editorial;
  • expert;
  • institutional;
  • normative;
  • technical;
  • ethical.

Each category is considered in accordance with the established procedure.

Chapter 4. Consideration of Conflicts

Consideration is carried out in the following stages:

  • registration of the application;
  • preliminary analysis;
  • determination of the competent body;
  • collection of materials;
  • expert consideration;
  • adoption of a decision;
  • registration of the result;
  • oversight of execution.

All stages are documented.

Chapter 5. Principle of Conflict Minimisation

The principal task of Chronicle OS is not to identify a culpable party, but to restore the resilience of institutional interaction.

Where a conflict may be eliminated through normative clarification or organisational improvement, the corresponding changes are prepared simultaneously with the consideration of the specific case.

Chapter 6. Appeals

Each participant in Chronicle OS has the right to an appellate review of a decision.

An appeal is considered by an independent competent body. That body did not participate in the initial consideration of the matter.

The results of the appeal are recorded in the Chronicle Registry.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

Institutional audit is carried out on the basis of the following principles:

  • independence;
  • objectivity;
  • reproducibility;
  • normative certainty;
  • documentation;
  • the development of institutional quality.

The subject of assessment comprises processes and institutional organisation.

Chapter 3. Objects of Audit

The following may be subject to audit:

  • a University Chronicle Node;
  • editorial procedures;
  • expert procedures;
  • digital infrastructure;
  • registration processes;
  • the Documentation System;

  • the quality of institutional interaction.

Chapter 4. Types of Audit

GOV-012 — INSTITUTIONAL RISK MANAGEMENT FRAMEWORK

Document ID: GOV-012

Document Type: Institutional Risk Management Framework

Hierarchy: Operational Governance

Status: MASTER TEMPLATE

Edition: Master Edition

Version: 1.0

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the system for identifying, assessing, preventing and monitoring institutional risks of Chronicle OS.

An institutional risk is understood as an event or a combination of circumstances. Such a risk is capable of adversely affecting the resilience of Chronicle OS, of a University Chronicle Node or of international scholarly communication.

Chapter 2. Principles of Risk Management

Risk management is carried out on the basis of the following principles:

  • prevention is preferable to the elimination of consequences;
  • risk assessment is carried out before a decision is adopted;
  • every risk has a designated responsible owner;
  • risks are documented;
  • risk is regarded as an object of governance rather than as an emergency event.

Chapter 3. Categories of Institutional Risks

The following categories of risk are distinguished within Chronicle OS:

Architectural — relate to a violation of the integrity of the Documentation System.

Academic — relate to a violation of scholarly integrity.

Organisational — arise as a consequence of a violation of governance procedures.

Digital — relate to the functioning of the digital infrastructure.

Reputational — affect the level of international trust in Chronicle OS and University Chronicle Nodes.

Legal — arise as a consequence of non-compliance with applicable legislation or normative documentation.

Chapter 4. Risk Life Cycle

Every risk passes through the following stages:

  • identification;
  • registration;
  • analysis;
  • assessment of likelihood;
  • assessment of consequences;
  • development of response measures;
  • oversight of execution;
  • closure of the risk.

All stages are recorded in the Chronicle Registry.

Chapter 5. Institutional Risk Register

Chronicle OS maintains a unified Register of Institutional Risks.

For each risk, the following are indicated:

  • the identifier;
  • the description;
  • the category;
  • the source of origin;
  • the likelihood;
  • the potential consequences;
  • the responsible coordinator;
  • the current status;
  • the measures taken.

The Register is used as an instrument of strategic governance.

Chapter 6. Resilience Management

The principal task of the risk management system is to ensure the institutional resilience of Chronicle OS.

Every identified risk is regarded as a basis for the improvement of:

  • the normative framework;
  • the digital infrastructure;
  • the organisational model;
  • operational procedures;
  • the Documentation System.

Risk management is not regarded as a reaction to crisis. Risk management is a permanent mechanism for the development of the international institutional system.

Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the life cycle of a University Chronicle Node within the international institutional ecosystem of Chronicle OS.

The life cycle describes the sequence of states of an institutional node from the moment of connection to archival status.

Chapter 2. Life Cycle Stages

Each University Chronicle Node passes through the following stages:

  • initiation;
  • institutional integration;
  • operation;
  • development;
  • transformation;
  • archival status.

Transition between stages is effected in accordance with the Documentation System.

Chapter 3. Initiation

The initiation stage includes:

  • the establishment of institutional contact;
  • a preliminary assessment of conformity;
  • the preparation of the foundational documentation package;
  • the agreement of the interaction model;
  • the adoption of the decision to commence integration.

At this stage, the University Chronicle Node is not yet an active element of Chronicle OS.

Chapter 4. Institutional Integration

At the integration stage, the following are carried out:

  • connection to the Chronicle Registry;
  • registration of institutional identifiers;
  • deployment of the Chronicle Node;
  • formation of the editorial structure;
  • connection of the expert network;
  • commissioning of the digital infrastructure.

Upon completion of integration, the University Chronicle Node acquires the status of an active institutional participant.

Chapter 5. Operation and Development

At the operation stage, the following are carried out:

  • editorial activity;
  • the conduct of expert procedures;
  • international cooperation;
  • institutional audit;
  • the improvement of processes;
  • the implementation of new editions of the Documentation System.

Development is regarded as a natural component of the operation stage.

Chapter 6. Transformation and Archival Status

Where the organisational model of a University Chronicle Node changes, the Node may enter a stage of institutional transformation.

Where activity ceases, transition to archival status is effected.

Archival status provides for:

  • the preservation of registration records;
  • the preservation of normative history;
  • the preservation of publication information;
  • the preservation of institutional memory.

Archiving is not regarded as the elimination of institutional knowledge.
