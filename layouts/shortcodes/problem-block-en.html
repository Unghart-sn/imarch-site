The Problem: Institutional Vacuum

Today, doing good is legally painful. There are no ready-made solutions for legal operation, no rules for managing trust, and every project drowns in chaos, built from scratch.

No "boxed solutions" for mission legalization

You have to fight through bureaucratic hell alone, instead of focusing on what matters: your mission.

Today, to legally do good work (help, teach, heal, create), you must endure bureaucratic hell alone: sole proprietorship, taxes, reporting.

There is no ready-made "construction kit" that provides a legal shell for your mission "out of the box," leaving you to focus on the mission itself.

No protocols for trust management and succession

Any project falls apart when money appears or the leader leaves. There are no rules that automatically resolve disputes and pass the mission to successors.

When money, partners, or apprentices enter a project, the "disassembly" begins: who invested how much, who's in charge, who gets what. There are no ready-made, proven rules (protocols) that automatically resolve these disputes.

And if the leader leaves, the project dies. There is no mechanism for handing over the mission to someone who will truly continue it — not just a relative.
