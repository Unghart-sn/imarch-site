> Entropy in physics is a measure of disorder. Energy dissipates, the system moves toward chaos. In social projects, it's the same: 90% of energy goes not to the mission, but to overcoming the institutional vacuum.

You open an educational center. Your neighbor in another city also opens an educational center. You **don't collaborate — you compete** for the same solutions to the same problems.

## The Bicycle. Again.

Every social entrepreneur:
1.  Independently figures out legal forms
2.  Creates a team motivation system from scratch  
3.  Invents ways to attract resources
4.  Makes the same mistakes as thousands before them

**This isn't development. It's monkey labor.** Giant energy of human potential is spent on **repeating what's already been done**.

## Why Does This Happen?

There's no **open institutional infrastructure**. No:
*   Libraries of proven legal structures
*   Standardized management protocols
*   Shared knowledge bases and tools
*   Ecosystem of mutual support

**Every man an island.** Everyone for themselves. The result — **colossal efficiency losses**.

## Burnout Economy

A social entrepreneur spends:
*   **40% of time** on bureaucracy and legal issues
*   **30% of time** resolving internal conflicts
*   **20% of time** searching for resources
*   **10% of time** on the actual mission

And that's in the best case. More often — **burns out and closes** at the 60% stage.

> "I wanted to save forests, but became an expert on taxes for NPOs. And still made mistakes in reporting." (Eco-activist, 2 years)

## Infrastructure Hunger

Imagine if every programmer:
*   Wrote their own compiler
*   Created their own database
*   Designed their own operating system

**No project would ever be completed.** This is exactly what happens in the social sphere.

## What's the Outcome?

**Social entropy:** transformation of creative energy into friction heat against bureaucracy, conflicts, and duplication.

We lose:
*   **Innovation** (all energy goes to basic issues)
*   **Scale** (projects remain local)
*   **Resilience** (team burnout)
*   **Speed of change** (the world changes faster than we can adapt)

**We don't need another initiative. We need an operating system for initiatives.**
