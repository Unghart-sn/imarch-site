> You've gathered a team of like-minded people. At first everything is perfect: shared values, burning eyes, sleepless nights for the idea. Then the first money appears. Or the leader decides to leave. And it begins: "Who's in charge here?", "Who invested how much?", "Who gets what?"

Familiar? This isn't a people problem. It's a problem of **missing institutional protocols**.

## Spontaneity vs. System

At the beginning of any social project, all relationships are built on trust and enthusiasm. It's beautiful, but **fragile as glass**.

**Without documented rules:**
*   The first conflict of interest tears the team apart
*   The departure of a key person kills the project
*   The inheritance goes not to the one who will continue the mission, but to the one who shouts louder or is legally "more right"

You're creating not a project, but a **time bomb**.

## Why Doesn't "Just Trust Each Other" Work?

Because trust is an emotion. And emotions are changeable. Today you're "soul brothers," tomorrow — competitors for resources.

**Institutional protocols are the translation of human relationships into the language of rules.** Like a constitution for a state. It doesn't assume everyone will be friends. It guarantees that even enemies will play by common rules.

> "Trust is a luxury no serious project can afford. It needs guarantees." (Fintech startup founder)

## Three Breaking Points of Any Project

1.  **Money.** As soon as the first profit (or investment) appears, division begins. No algorithm — war starts.
2.  **Power.** Who makes decisions? By what right? What if the leader makes a mistake? No correction mechanism — the project goes off a cliff.
3.  **Succession.** The founder leaves. Who inherits the work? Children? Friends? Or the one who will actually continue the mission? No rules — the project dies.

**Each of these points is an institutional hole where 80% of social initiatives fall through.**

## Protocols Are Not Bureaucracy

They are **anti-fragility**. A system that:
*   **Automatically** resolves conflicts (not through arguments, but through pre-defined algorithms)
*   **Protects** the mission from human factors
*   **Guarantees** continuity regardless of who leaves
*   **Transforms** chaos into predictability

**Imagine:** Project participants know ALL rules from day one. How profits are distributed. How decisions are made. How authority is transferred. No unspoken agreements. No behind-the-scenes games. There's a codebook. Play by the rules.

## What Do We Lose Without Protocols?

We lose **resilience**. One-day projects that flare up and fade. Talented people who become disillusioned with "collaborative creation." Missions that die with their founders.

We build **card houses** in a world that needs **fortresses**.
