# Problem: What Happens After the Mission Is Officially Over

When a military member returns home or a person retires after years of service, from the outside it looks like "demobilization" or "well-deserved rest."

Inside, another process often begins — **deactivation stress**.

The person loses their familiar rhythm, team, daily structure, sense of purpose.  
To this are added traumatic memories, bodily tension, fear of the future, and the hidden fatigue of the family.

---

## 🧠 Chronic Stress and PTSD

For many veterans and their loved ones, reality looks like this:

- intrusive memories and flashbacks;  
- anxiety, irritability, anger outbursts;  
- sleep disorders, nightmares, fatigue that "sleep can't fix";  
- bodily symptoms: pain, cramps, blood pressure issues, breathing problems, digestive disorders.

Even when the person is officially "home" — the psyche continues to live in threat mode.

---

## 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 Family Burden

It's not only the veteran who suffers, but also their surroundings:

- the partner or spouse lives in constant tension;  
- children intuitively sense the adults' state but don't understand its causes;  
- relationships shift into survival mode, not growth mode.

The family simultaneously becomes **the only front of support**  
and **the zone of greatest tension**.

Without accessible help tools "for home, for family," this burden only grows.

---

## 🧩 Inaccessibility and Fragmentation of Help

Even when help formally exists, in practice there are barriers:

- quality psychotherapy and rehabilitation are often too expensive;  
- remote regions simply lack specialists of the needed profile;  
- stigma prevents "going to a psychologist" or "complaining about weakness";  
- most programs are not integrated into the family's daily life.  

As a result:

- some veterans never reach help;  
- some drop programs without seeing results;  
- some remain in a state of "frozen crisis" for years.

---

## 🔄 The Vicious Cycle

Without systemic support, a typical scenario unfolds:

1. The veteran returns or leaves the system.  
2. Stress and tension accumulate but are not processed.  
3. The family burns out, irritation and distance grow.  
4. Related problems increase: addictions, conflicts, exacerbation of chronic illnesses.  
5. The support system intervenes too late and too narrowly, without changing the basic scenario.

This creates a **hidden wave of loss** — people who physically survived but gradually drop out of active life.

---

## 🕒 Why It's Important to Act Now

This problem won't "resolve itself."  
Each year of delay:

- increases the number of people with chronic psychosomatic conditions;  
- raises the burden on the healthcare and social support systems;  
- strengthens the feeling of helplessness in families;  
- undermines trust in institutions that "were supposed to help."

If we don't offer **simple, accessible, and reproducible support practices**,  
we risk losing an entire generation of people who have experience, strength, and potential.

---

## 💬 What Needs to Change

The problem isn't only in the lack of resources, but in the format of help.

We need solutions that:

- work **inside the family and community**, not only in a specialist's office;  
- rely on **bodily and psycho-emotional practices** that are understandable and reproducible;  
- provide quick, tangible results so people gain faith in change;  
- are embedded in a **transparent tracking and feedback system**, so donors and partners see real impact.
