### 1. Introduction: The Axiom of the 'Primary Accreditor'

In legal theory and institutional economics, the key question is: **who confers authority upon a system that creates a fundamentally new standard?**

Historical analysis demonstrates that none of humanity's foundational institutions — from the universities of Bologna and Oxford in the twelfth century to the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) and cryptographic certification centres (Root CA) in the twentieth and twenty-first centuries — received external permissive sanctions at the stage of their creation.

The legitimacy of any Prime Source is founded not upon bureaucratic accreditation delegated from above, but upon the **triad of sovereignty**:

### 2. The Genesis of Authority: From the Bolognese Charter to the Root Certificate

#### 2.1. The Academic Precedent (Self-Governance)

The first European *Universitas* arose as autonomous corporations of scholars (guilds). Their primary authority was based exclusively upon their internal charter and the high quality of the knowledge they transmitted.

Only decades later was their *de facto* status formalised by royal and papal bulls.

Thus, **the practice of application and the internal coherence of a standard are primary in relation to external institutional recognition**.

#### 2.2. The Technological and Cryptographic Precedent (Root CA)

In contemporary information networks, security rests upon Root Certificates.

A root certification authority is **self-signed**: no one accredits it 'above', since it is itself the base point of trust for all subordinate nodes.

IMARCH LTD applies this principle to the sphere of intellectual property and R&D capitalisation, forming its own **Root Standard Originator**.

#### 3.1. Normative and Protocol Basis

The formed corpus of institutional regulations (including Protocols RN-0028, RN-0031 and Know-How Act RN-0038) establishes exhaustive rules for the assessment, depositing and handling of 'blind maps' of projects (Blind Mapping).

#### 3.2. Legal Sovereignty (The Presumption of Priority)

The fixation of authors' rights and know-how under the jurisdiction of England and Wales (within the framework of the international norms of TRIPS and the Berne Convention) creates a **presumption** of authenticity and international priority.

In contrast to delegated powers, what operates here is the presumption of legality of the very act of establishing the standard.

#### 3.3. The Multi-Cluster Node Model (Federated Node Network)

The accession of university centres and R&D subdivisions to the platform as independent nodes (for example, `umss.imarch.sbs`) legally and factually signifies their agreement with the Primary Standard of IMARCH LTD and their transition to a unified validation regulation.

### 4. The Economic Metaphor of the Standard

Renouncing any attachment to physical assets (as was the case in the era of the 'Gold Standard'), IMARCH LTD proposes a model in which the base of value is constituted by the **quality of the intellectual metric**.

This aligns the new infrastructural standard with the digital protocols of the twenty-first century, in which value is generated not by an emission centre, but by a network of validating nodes.
