### PREAMBLE

Operational Governance defines the governance system of Chronicle OS as an international institutional infrastructure for scholarly communication.

This Volume establishes the principles governing the distribution of authority, responsibility and managerial decision-making. These principles ensure the sustainable operation of Chronicle OS irrespective of the scale of the system or the number of participants.

All documents of this Volume form an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and are applied in conjunction with Volume I (Institutional Codex) and Volume II (Institutional Relations Package).

### GOV-001 — CHRONICLE OS GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK

**Document ID:** GOV-001

**Document Type:** Governance Framework

**Hierarchy:** Operational Governance

**Status:** MASTER TEMPLATE

**Edition:** Master Edition

**Version:** 1.0

<a id="gov-001-chapter-1"></a>
#### Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the governance system of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system of scholarly communication.

The document establishes the principles governing the distribution of authority, responsibility and managerial decision-making. These principles ensure the sustainable operation of Chronicle OS irrespective of the scale of the system or the number of participants.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Governance Objectives

The Chronicle OS governance system ensures:

- institutional resilience;
- coherence of action among all participants;
- preservation of the autonomy of University Chronicle Nodes;
- compliance with the normative framework;
- continuous development of the international ecosystem;
- protection of the long-term interests of the scholarly community.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-3"></a>
#### Chapter 3. Fundamental Governance Principles

Chronicle OS governance is founded upon the following principles.

**3.1. Institutional Primacy**

The interests of the institution take precedence. The interests of individual officials, units or organisations do not take precedence over the interests of the institution.

**3.2. Distributed Agency**

Each University Chronicle Node retains its own institutional autonomy.

Chronicle OS coordinates interaction. Chronicle OS does not replace the internal governance of the university.

**3.3. Delegation of Responsibility**

Authority is delegated to the level at which the relevant decision may be taken most professionally and efficiently.

**3.4. Normative Governance**

Every managerial decision is grounded in the applicable normative documentation of Chronicle OS.

Personal preferences, traditions or situational circumstances do not replace established procedures.

**3.5. Architectural Consistency**

The development of individual Chronicle OS components does not compromise the integrity of the entire institutional architecture.

Every change is assessed from the standpoint of its impact upon the system as a whole.

**3.6. Transparency of Accountability**

Every governance function has a designated bearer of responsibility.

No process may exist without an established level of accountability.

**3.7. Evolutionary Development**

Chronicle OS is regarded as a continuously evolving institutional system.

Procedures are improved through the progressive refinement of the normative framework. Such refinement does not destroy the previously established architecture.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-4"></a>
#### Chapter 4. Governance Levels of Chronicle OS

The Chronicle OS governance system is organised as a distributed multi-level architecture.

Each level possesses its own competence. No level substitutes the functions of another.

Governance is built upon the principle of distributed responsibility. The unity of the normative framework is preserved.

The following governance levels are distinguished within Chronicle OS:

- the International Level;
- the Institutional Level;
- the Local Level;
- the Operational Level.

Each level operates within the limits of its own competence. The levels interact through approved protocols.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-5"></a>
#### Chapter 5. International Governance Level

The International Level ensures the strategic development of Chronicle OS.

Its competence includes:

- development of the institutional architecture;
- approval of normative documentation;
- formulation of international policy;
- development of international partnership;
- coordination of long-term programmes;
- ensuring the unity of the normative environment;
- development of the Chronicle Registry;
- maintaining the integrity of the Documentation System.

The International Level does not exercise operational governance over universities.

Its task is to maintain the resilience of the entire international ecosystem.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-6"></a>
#### Chapter 6. Institutional Governance Level

The Institutional Level is represented by the University Chronicle Node.

Each University Chronicle Node independently organises:

- editorial activity;
- internal procedures;
- interaction with authors;
- interaction with experts;
- organisation of the publication process;
- local digital infrastructure;
- development of university scholarly communication.

The Institutional Level adopts decisions independently. Such decisions do not contravene the normative framework of Chronicle OS.

Chronicle OS does not interfere in the academic autonomy of the university, provided that the activity of the University Chronicle Node complies with the applicable normative documentation.

Each University Chronicle Node bears responsibility for the quality of its own institutional activity. The Node simultaneously participates in the formation of the shared space of international scholarly trust.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-7"></a>
#### Chapter 7. Operational Governance Level

The Operational Level ensures the direct execution of the day-to-day processes of Chronicle OS.

The Operational Level comprises:

- the Editor-in-Chief;
- the Managing Editor;
- the Editorial Secretary;
- the Expert Network Coordinator;
- the Chronicle Registry Operator;
- the Technical Editor;
- the System Administrator of the University Chronicle Node;
- other officials determined by the internal structure of the university.

The Operational Level implements approved procedures. The Operational Level does not modify the normative architecture of the system.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-8"></a>
#### Chapter 8. Distribution of Authority

Authority is distributed according to the principle of functional competence.

Strategic matters are decided at the International Level.

Institutional matters are decided by the University Chronicle Node.

Operational matters are decided by the officials responsible for the relevant processes.

Delegation of authority is permitted within the limits of the established normative framework.

The concentration of incompatible functions in a single participant is not permitted where this may affect the objectivity of decisions taken.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-9"></a>
#### Chapter 9. Managerial Decision-Making

All managerial decisions of Chronicle OS are classified into the following categories:

**Strategic decisions** — determine the long-term development of the international system.

**Institutional decisions** — relate to the organisation of the activity of the University Chronicle Node.

**Operational decisions** — ensure the execution of daily working processes.

**Architectural decisions** — relate to changes in the Chronicle OS normative framework, the development of the Documentation System, the Chronicle Registry and other foundational components of the system.

Architectural decisions are adopted following an analysis of their impact upon the integrity of the entire institutional model.

Every decision:

- is documented;
- is traceable;
- is reproducible;
- complies with the applicable Chronicle OS normative framework.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-10"></a>
#### Chapter 10. Architectural Oversight

Architectural oversight ensures the preservation of the integrity of Chronicle OS as an international institutional system.

The subject of architectural oversight comprises:

- normative documentation;
- organisational structure;
- digital infrastructure;
- institutional procedures;
- mechanisms of interaction among participants;
- development of the Documentation System.

Architectural oversight does not replace operational governance.

Its task is to ensure that changes correspond to the general architecture of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-11"></a>
#### Chapter 11. Change Management

Chronicle OS regards change as a natural part of institutional development.

Every change complies with the following requirements:

- it has a documented justification;
- it takes account of its impact upon the existing normative framework;
- it preserves compatibility with previously adopted decisions;
- it ensures traceability of changes;
- it does not compromise the institutional resilience of the system.

Changes to normative documentation are effected through the official versioning of the Documentation System.

Each edition becomes part of the institutional memory of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-001-chapter-12"></a>
#### Chapter 12. Final Provisions

The Framework is applied in conjunction with:

- the Chronicle OS Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
- the Documentation System;
- the Institutional Relations Package (see Volume II);
- Operational Procedures;
- Chronicle Registry Standards (see Volume I);
- the Certification Framework (see Volume II, INS-007).

The document defines the foundational governance model of Chronicle OS. The document is the basis for the development of operational procedures, working regulations and digital scenarios for the functioning of the international institutional ecosystem.

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

### GOV-002 — INTERNATIONAL GOVERNANCE STRUCTURE

**Document ID:** GOV-002

**Document Type:** International Governance Structure

**Hierarchy:** Operational Governance

**Status:** MASTER TEMPLATE

**Edition:** Master Edition

**Version:** 1.0

<a id="gov-002-chapter-1"></a>
#### Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the international organisational structure of Chronicle OS. The document establishes the system for the distribution of functions among international governance bodies and determines their competence.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Principle of Distributed Governance

Chronicle OS does not employ a centralised administrative model.

International governance is exercised through the interaction of specialised bodies. Each body is responsible for its own area of competence.

No governance body possesses absolute authority.

The system is built upon the principle of mutual institutional balance.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-3"></a>
#### Chapter 3. International Institutional Level

The highest level of international governance ensures:

- preservation of the institutional architecture of Chronicle OS;
- development of the normative framework;
- international coordination;
- protection of the long-term interests of the international ecosystem.

This level does not exercise operational governance over universities and does not interfere in their academic activity.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-4"></a>
#### Chapter 4. International Architecture Council

The International Architecture Council undertakes:

- development of the Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
- development of the Documentation System;
- approval of new normative documents;
- consideration of architectural changes;
- oversight of the compatibility of new decisions with the existing institutional model.

Decisions of the Council are adopted on the basis of the normative documentation of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-5"></a>
#### Chapter 5. International Editorial Council

The International Editorial Council ensures:

- development of international editorial policy;
- coordination of the activity of editorial boards;
- development of Publication Ethics (see Volume I, DOC-004);
- support of international scholarly programmes;
- development of the quality of scholarly communication.

The Editorial Council does not interfere in the independent peer review of individual publications.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-6"></a>
#### Chapter 6. International Expert Council

The International Expert Council is responsible for:

- development of the Chronicle OS expert network;
- formation of criteria for expert activity;
- development of reviewer training programmes;
- evaluation of the professional standards of the expert community.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-7"></a>
#### Chapter 7. International Digital Infrastructure Council

The Digital Infrastructure Council is responsible for the development of the technological environment of Chronicle OS.

Its competence comprises:

- development of the Chronicle Registry;
- development of the Chronicle Node;
- digital identification of participants;
- integration of international services;
- ensuring the compatibility of digital components;
- support of the Documentation System in the digital environment;
- development of tools for the automation of scholarly communication.

The Council ensures the technological resilience of the international ecosystem.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-8"></a>
#### Chapter 8. International Certification Council

The Certification Council undertakes:

- development of criteria for institutional evaluation;
- development of Chronicle Certification standards;
- support of audit procedures;
- confirmation of the compliance of University Chronicle Nodes with the requirements of Chronicle OS;
- improvement of institutional maturity models.

The Council acts independently of editorial bodies.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-9"></a>
#### Chapter 9. Coordination of Council Activities

International Councils interact on the basis of the approved normative documentation.

Decisions are adopted within the limits of their own competence.

Where matters affecting several areas of activity are considered, temporary joint working groups are formed.

No Council may alter the competence of another Council.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-10"></a>
#### Chapter 10. Principle of Collegiality

Strategic decisions are adopted collegially.

In the discussion, account is taken of:

- scholarly consequences;
- institutional consequences;
- architectural consequences;
- technological consequences;
- international consequences.

Collegiality is regarded as a mechanism for ensuring the resilience of the decisions adopted.

<a id="gov-002-chapter-11"></a>
#### Chapter 11. Principle of Institutional Independence

Each international governance body exercises its activity independently of commercial, political and other external interests.

The principal criteria for decision-making are:

- scholarly integrity;
- institutional resilience;
- compliance with the Documentation System;
- compliance with the Institutional Codex (see Volume I).

<a id="gov-002-chapter-12"></a>
#### Chapter 12. Final Provisions

The document is applied in conjunction with:

- the Chronicle OS Institutional Codex (see Volume I);
- the Documentation System;
- the Governance Framework (GOV-001);
- the Institutional Relations Package (see Volume II);
- Chronicle Registry Standards (see Volume I).

The structure of international governance of Chronicle OS may develop as the international network grows. Such development is carried out without violating the principles of the distributed institutional architecture.

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

### GOV-003 — INSTITUTIONAL ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

**Document ID:** GOV-003

**Document Type:** Institutional Roles and Responsibilities

**Hierarchy:** Operational Governance

**Status:** MASTER TEMPLATE

**Edition:** Master Edition

**Version:** 1.0

<a id="gov-003-chapter-1"></a>
#### Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the principal institutional roles of participants in Chronicle OS. The document establishes the limits of their competence and distributes responsibility among the levels of governance of the international ecosystem.

The document is the normative basis for the subsequent development of operational procedures and working regulations.

<a id="gov-003-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Principle of Functional Role

In Chronicle OS, responsibility is determined not by position, but by the function performed.

A single natural person may perform several roles, provided that this does not violate the principles of independence, objectivity and separation of authority.

The same role may be performed by different persons in different University Chronicle Nodes.

<a id="gov-003-chapter-3"></a>
#### Chapter 3. System Architect

The System Architect is responsible for the development of the institutional model of Chronicle OS.

Their competence includes:

- development of the architecture of the Documentation System;
- harmonisation of the normative framework;
- ensuring the compatibility of new components;

The document defines the system for identifying, assessing, preventing and monitoring institutional risks of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-012-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Principles of Risk Management

Risk management is carried out on the basis of the following principles:

- prevention is preferable to the elimination of consequences;
- risk assessment is carried out before a decision is adopted;
- every risk has a designated responsible owner;
- risks are documented;
- risk is regarded as an object of governance rather than as an emergency event.

<a id="gov-012-chapter-3"></a>

The following categories of risk are distinguished within Chronicle OS:

**Architectural** — relate to a violation of the integrity of the Documentation System.

**Academic** — relate to a violation of scholarly integrity.

**Organisational** — arise as a consequence of a violation of governance procedures.

**Digital** — relate to the functioning of the digital infrastructure.

**Reputational** — affect the level of international trust in Chronicle OS and University Chronicle Nodes.

**Legal** — arise as a consequence of non-compliance with applicable legislation or normative documentation.

<a id="gov-012-chapter-4"></a>
#### Chapter 4. Risk Life Cycle

Every risk passes through the following stages:

- identification;
- registration;
- analysis;
- assessment of likelihood;
- assessment of consequences;
- development of response measures;
- oversight of execution;
- closure of the risk.

All stages are recorded in the Chronicle Registry.

Chronicle OS maintains a unified Register of Institutional Risks.

For each risk, the following are indicated:

- the identifier;
- the description;
- the category;
- the source of origin;
- the likelihood;
- the potential consequences;
- the responsible coordinator;
- the current status;
- the measures taken.

The Register is used as an instrument of strategic governance.

<a id="gov-012-chapter-6"></a>
#### Chapter 6. Resilience Management

The principal task of the risk management system is to ensure the institutional resilience of Chronicle OS.

Every identified risk is regarded as a basis for the improvement of:

- the normative framework;
- the digital infrastructure;
- the organisational model;
- operational procedures;
- the Documentation System.

### GOV-013 — INSTITUTIONAL PERFORMANCE FRAMEWORK

**Document ID:** GOV-013

**Document Type:** Institutional Performance Framework

**Hierarchy:** Operational Governance

**Status:** MASTER TEMPLATE

**Edition:** Master Edition

**Version:** 1.0

<a id="gov-013-chapter-1"></a>
#### Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the system for evaluating the effectiveness of the functioning of Chronicle OS and of University Chronicle Nodes.

The evaluation of effectiveness is applied for the purposes of institutional development. Evaluation is not regarded as a mechanism of administrative influence.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Principle of Institutional Performance

The effectiveness of Chronicle OS is determined neither by the volume of published materials nor by quantitative indicators of activity.

The principal criterion of effectiveness is the quality of resilient international scholarly communication. Communication is ensured by the institutional system.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-3"></a>
#### Chapter 3. Areas of Evaluation

Institutional effectiveness is evaluated in the following areas:

- normative maturity;
- editorial resilience;
- the quality of expert activity;
- the development of international cooperation;
- digital maturity;
- the quality of institutional governance;
- the development of the scholarly community;
- the reproducibility of processes.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-4"></a>
#### Chapter 4. Institutional Indicators

In the conduct of evaluation, the following groups of indicators may be used.

**Organisational indicators** — reflect the resilience of governance processes.

**Academic indicators** — reflect the quality of scholarly communication.

**Technological indicators** — reflect the maturity of the digital infrastructure.

**International indicators** — reflect the level of integration of a University Chronicle Node into the international Chronicle OS ecosystem.

**Institutional indicators** — reflect compliance with the Documentation System and the Institutional Codex.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-5"></a>
#### Chapter 5. Use of Evaluation Results

The results of institutional evaluation are used for:

- the preparation of development programmes;
- the improvement of the normative framework;
- the preparation of recommendations;
- the improvement of digital infrastructure;
- the development of the certification system;
- the preparation of new editions of the Documentation System.

Evaluation of effectiveness is not a basis for restricting the academic autonomy of a University Chronicle Node.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-6"></a>
#### Chapter 6. Principle of Maturity

Chronicle OS regards institutional maturity as a continuous process.

The enhancement of maturity is achieved through:

- the accumulation of experience;
- the improvement of the Documentation System;
- the development of digital infrastructure;
- the expansion of international cooperation;
- the improvement of procedures;
- the training of personnel;
- institutional learning.

Institutional maturity is regarded as a strategic characteristic of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-013-chapter-7"></a>
#### Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

### GOV-014 — INSTITUTIONAL LIFECYCLE FRAMEWORK

**Document ID:** GOV-014

**Document Type:** Institutional Lifecycle Framework

**Hierarchy:** Operational Governance

**Status:** MASTER TEMPLATE

**Edition:** Master Edition

**Version:** 1.0

<a id="gov-014-chapter-1"></a>
#### Chapter 1. Purpose of the Document

The document defines the life cycle of a University Chronicle Node within the international institutional ecosystem of Chronicle OS.

The life cycle describes the sequence of states of an institutional node from the moment of connection to archival status.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-2"></a>
#### Chapter 2. Life Cycle Stages

Each University Chronicle Node passes through the following stages:

- initiation;
- institutional integration;
- operation;
- development;
- transformation;
- archival status.

Transition between stages is effected in accordance with the Documentation System.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-3"></a>
#### Chapter 3. Initiation

The initiation stage includes:

- the establishment of institutional contact;
- a preliminary assessment of conformity;
- the preparation of the foundational documentation package;
- the agreement of the interaction model;
- the adoption of the decision to commence integration.

At this stage, the University Chronicle Node is not yet an active element of Chronicle OS.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-4"></a>
#### Chapter 4. Institutional Integration

At the integration stage, the following are carried out:

- connection to the Chronicle Registry;
- registration of institutional identifiers;
- deployment of the Chronicle Node;
- formation of the editorial structure;
- connection of the expert network;
- commissioning of the digital infrastructure.

Upon completion of integration, the University Chronicle Node acquires the status of an active institutional participant.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-5"></a>
#### Chapter 5. Operation and Development

At the operation stage, the following are carried out:

- editorial activity;
- the conduct of expert procedures;
- international cooperation;
- institutional audit;
- the improvement of processes;
- the implementation of new editions of the Documentation System.

Development is regarded as a natural component of the operation stage.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-6"></a>
#### Chapter 6. Transformation and Archival Status

Where the organisational model of a University Chronicle Node changes, the Node may enter a stage of institutional transformation.

Where activity ceases, transition to archival status is effected.

Archival status provides for:

- the preservation of registration records;
- the preservation of normative history;
- the preservation of publication information;
- the preservation of institutional memory.

Archiving is not regarded as the elimination of institutional knowledge.

<a id="gov-014-chapter-7"></a>
#### Chapter 7. Final Provisions

This document forms an integral part of the Chronicle OS Documentation System and is applied in conjunction with the other normative documents of the system.

The document concludes the third volume of the normative documentation of Chronicle OS. The document defines the integrated model of operational governance of the international institutional system of scholarly communication.

The document consolidates the provisions of all documents of Operational Governance into a single architectural system.

The operational model of Chronicle OS is founded upon the following interconnected components:

- the Governance Framework (GOV-001);

The aggregate of these documents forms the unified Operational Governance system of Chronicle OS.

Operational Governance ensures:

- the resilience of the international institutional architecture;
- the uniformity of processes;
- the reproducibility of activity;

All documents of Operational Governance are regarded as interconnected elements of a single normative system.

The history of changes forms part of the institutional memory of Chronicle OS.

### UNIFIED GLOSSARY OF TERMS

**Chronicle OS** — an international institutional system of scholarly communication.

**Chronicle OS Documentation System** — the aggregate of normative documents defining the architecture, governance and operation of Chronicle OS.

**Institutional Codex** — the foundational constitutional document of Chronicle OS, defining its identity, principles and foundational architecture.

**Deployment Framework** — the normative document defining the procedure for the deployment of Chronicle OS within universities and research organisations.

**Operational Governance** — the governance system of Chronicle OS, defining the distribution of authority, responsibility and decision-making procedures.

**University Chronicle Node** — a local institutional circuit of Chronicle OS deployed within a university or research organisation.

**Chronicle Registry** — the institutional registry of Chronicle OS, ensuring the registration, identification and preservation of institutional memory.

**Documentation System** — the system of normative documentation of Chronicle OS, comprising all volumes and documents of the corpus.

**International Editorial Office** — the international governance body ensuring the development of editorial policy and the coordination of editorial boards.

**International Expert Network** — the international network of scholarly experts ensuring independent expert assessment and the development of international scholarly communication.

**International Architecture Council** — the international governance body ensuring the development of the institutional architecture and normative framework of Chronicle OS.

**International Certification Council** — the international governance body undertaking the certification of University Chronicle Nodes and the development of institutional assessment standards.

**International Digital Infrastructure Council** — the international governance body responsible for the development of the technological environment of Chronicle OS.

**Institutional Audit** — the procedure for assessing the compliance of the activity of a University Chronicle Node with the requirements of the Documentation System and the Institutional Codex.

**Institutional Risk** — an event or a combination of circumstances capable of adversely affecting the resilience of Chronicle OS or of a University Chronicle Node.

**Institutional Context** — the aggregate of normative, organisational, digital and scholarly interconnections determining the state of the international ecosystem of scholarly communication.

**Operational Governance Summary** — the consolidated document uniting all components of Operational Governance into a single architectural system.
