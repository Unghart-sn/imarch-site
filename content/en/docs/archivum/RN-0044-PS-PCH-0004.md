#### 1. I-4 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-4. Academic Romanticism

#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-4
**Series:** I
**Name:** Academic Romanticism
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**Ancient ruins + marble + frescoes + nineteenth-century painting + academic still life + IMARCH institutional artefacts.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of the ancient world nor a trite Neoclassical styling, but a visual language in its own right, in which eternity, wisdom, erudition and serenity exist as elements of a single institutional tradition.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-4:

> **Eternity and knowledge are not the past, but an active institutional force that preserves value across the centuries.**

Ancient forms are perceived as bearers of order, measure and academic memory.

Marble, frescoes and painted canvases represent material and artistic continuity.

IMARCH institutional artefacts (scrolls, seals, folios, manifestos) join the classical heritage to the sovereign digital circuit.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**eternity / modernity**
**wisdom / technology**
**marble / parchment**
**ruin / institution**
**antiquity / academy**
**repose / document**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials:

**Marble**
Polished and patinated stone, architectural fragments, columns, capitals.

**Fresco**
Matte plaster, muted earth pigments, traces of time.

**Parchment and old paper**
The dense texture of scrolls, manuscripts and charters with wax seals.

**Nineteenth-century painting**
Oil texture, warm varnish, visible brushwork, academic layering.

**Brass and bronze**
Instruments, inkwells, scales, writing implements.

**Wax seal**
Red and dark sealing wax, impressions of heraldic and institutional marks.

Materials must look physically convincing: real stone, real paper, real painting, not a digital filter.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* ancient columns, capitals, pediments
* domes, rotundas, temple façades
* laurel branches and wreaths
* scrolls with sealing wax
* folios in leather bindings
* quill pens, inkwells
* astronomical instruments, globes, scales
* marble busts and statues
* painted canvases in gilded frames
* heraldic marks, monograms, IMARCH institutional emblems
* inscriptions in Latin and Greek
* DOI marks, verification keys, academic attributes

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and academic attribution, not as random decorative props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Monumentality**

Objects possess visual weight, stability and a sense of durability.

**Academic order**

The composition is built around a central document, scroll or architectural element.

**Classical geometry**

Proportions follow the canons of ancient and academic architecture: symmetry, clear axes, harmonic relations.

**Material hierarchy**

The principal object — a document, folio or architectural fragment — is subordinated to a single compositional logic.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of academic hierarchy:

**principal document or architectural element → writing implements and instruments → background environment (ruins, frescoes, canvases) → accent lighting details.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central axis
* frontal or slightly diagonal staging
* high visual stability
* controlled depth of space
* directed light highlighting the principal object
* a limited number of secondary details
* a background that works as context, not as a separate scene

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* warm tones: ochre, sepia, amber, gold, terracotta
* neutral tones: marble white, grey stone, dusty ochre

**Secondary palette:**

* deep green (laurel, frescoes)
* burgundy and dark red (seals, fabric)
* dark blue (sky, academic robes)

**Light accents:**

* a warm golden glow
* soft light on marble and parchment
* localized glow of wax seals and metal instruments

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with warm, muted tones.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is a bearer of meaning: it separates the eternal from the transient.

Preferred:

* soft directed light highlighting the principal object
* golden reflections on marble, brass and parchment
* contrast between a lit present and a shadowed background
* a background glow on the horizon — a sign of eternity and repose

Light must emphasize the dignity of the objects, not their drama.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-4 must create a sense of:

**eternity**
**wisdom**
**erudition**
**serenity**
**academic dignity**
**institutional continuity**
**material and cultural value**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Knowledge become an institution; eternity become a document.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human in I-4 is present **indirectly**:

* hands holding a quill or a document
* the silhouette of a scholar at work
* a marble bust or statue
* a figure in academic robes
* an allusion to nineteenth-century portraiture

The human figure retains the traits of restrained dignity, concentration and well-bred composure. The face may be absent — the emphasis falls on the gesture, the hand, the attribute.

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-4 is presented **in moderation and in an academic register**:

* DOI marks, verification keys, cryptographic signatures
* Chronicle OS digital interfaces, styled after ancient tablets and academic manifestos
* glowing crystals and optical instruments as a metaphor for transparency and authenticity

Technology must not dominate the classical layer. It is an **instrument for recording eternity**, not a theme in its own right.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-4 **is not**:

* a literal reconstruction of antiquity
* a museum display without modern context
* Neoclassical kitsch
* a fantasy academy
* steampunk with an excess of mechanisms
* cheap vintage styling
* a chaotic collage of ancient symbols

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If the classical heritage and the institutional circuit exist separately from one another, or degenerate into a caricatured collection of props, the work does not conform to I-4.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**witness**
the element carries the memory of the classical tradition;

**symbolic**
the element conveys status, eternity, academic meaning;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and holds the gaze.

Elements that exist for the sake of visual noise or meaningless accumulation of objects are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-4 permits a range from photorealistic painting to academic illustration.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**material credibility (marble, parchment, oil)**
**a warm, muted lighting scenario**
**academic clarity of composition**
**a symbolic layer of eternity and institutional continuity**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* certificates of authenticity
* sovereign mandates
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* academic monograph covers
* frontispieces
* illustrations for scholarly publications
* collector's editions

**Visual identity**

* the design of the IMARCH Bonus Boutique
* presentation materials
* premium cases
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* sovereign collector series
* musical visual concepts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-4 if it contains all of the following:

**1. A classical material layer (marble, parchment, oil, bronze).**

**2. A pronounced warm academic lighting scenario.**

**3. Institutional or technological elements of the IMARCH ecosystem.**

**4. A stable, balanced academic composition.**

**5. A sense of eternity, wisdom and institutional continuity.**

If only ancient props appear, unconnected to the ecosystem, it is not I-4.

If pure modern high-tech appears, without the classical material basis, it is not I-4.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **An academic still life in the spirit of the nineteenth century: marble fragments, ancient columns, parchment scrolls with sealing wax, folios in leather bindings, quill pens, brass instruments, painted canvases in gilded frames, laurel branches; warm muted light, golden reflections, a strict central composition, and a sense of eternity, wisdom, erudition and IMARCH institutional continuity.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-4 does not depict antiquity for the sake of decoration.**

**I-4 depicts eternity become an institution, and knowledge become a document.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
