#### 1. I-11 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-11. Imperial Neoclassicism

#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-11
**Series:** I
**Name:** Imperial Neoclassicism
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**Monumental oil portrait + classical symmetry + deep shadows + imperial blue and gold + IMARCH institutional environment.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of an imperial ceremonial portrait nor a trite Neoclassical styling, but a visual language in its own right, in which imperial grandeur, sovereign dignity and institutional power exist as elements of a single system.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-11:

> **Power is not an abstraction, but a visually legible institutional form, expressed through monumentality, symmetry and material.**

The human figure is perceived as a bearer of sovereign dignity, imperial will and institutional continuity.

Blue and gold represent nobility, eternity and imperial value.

Deep shadow and monumental symmetry convey stability, order and timeless power.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**power / restraint**
**grandeur / austerity**
**symmetry / depth**
**gold / shadow**
**empire / institution**
**eternity / moment**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials:

**Oil painting**
Dense brushwork, warm varnish texture, deep layering, visible canvas texture.

**Imperial blue**
Deep navy, royal blue, blue with gold veining.

**Gold**
Embossed frames, accessories, ornaments, buttons, watches.

**Wool and velvet**
Heavy fabrics, strict tailoring, deep dark tones.

**Oak and walnut**
A study environment, bookcases, desks, panelling.

**Leather and metal**
Briefcases, belts, watches, spectacles, writing implements.

Materials must look physically convincing: real oil painting, real wool, real gold, not a digital filter.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* monumental portraits in oil painting
* an imperial blue suit or mantle
* gold frames and ornaments
* academic and judicial robes
* laptops and tablets as instruments of power
* oak panelling and bookcases
* portraits of predecessors in gilded frames
* maps and globes
* family dynastic portraits
* architectural models and blueprints
* IMARCH institutional emblems
* gestures of willed control (hands on the desk, a quill in the hand)

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and institutional attribution, not as random decorative props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Monumentality**

The figure possesses visual weight, stability and a sense of imperial reliability.

**Classical symmetry**

The composition follows the canons of the ceremonial portrait: central axis, clear proportions, stable frames.

**Deep shadow**

Shadows create volume, depth and dramatic restraint.

**Material hierarchy**

The principal object — the person — is subordinated to a single compositional logic in which the environment supports status rather than distracting from it.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of imperial hierarchy:

**principal figure → imperial attributes (mantle, watch, quill) → study environment (panelling, portraits, cabinets) → background details.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central axis
* frontal staging
* high visual stability
* controlled depth of space
* directed light highlighting the face and hands
* a limited number of secondary details
* a background that works as context, not as a separate scene

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* imperial blue (navy, royal blue)
* dark grey and graphite
* dark brown and burgundy

**Secondary palette:**

* gold and brass
* warm wood (walnut, oak)
* cream and white (shirts, cuffs)

**Light accents:**

* a golden glow on the face and hands
* localized highlights on metal and gold
* contrast between a lit figure and the deep shadow of the background

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with imperial blue, gold and deep shadow.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is a bearer of imperial dignity.

Preferred:

* directed light highlighting the face and hands
* golden reflections on skin and fabric
* contrast between a lit figure and deep shadow
* localized glow of desk lamps and of portraits of predecessors

Light must emphasize the dignity and will of the figure, not its drama.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-11 must create a sense of:

**imperial grandeur**
**sovereign dignity**
**institutional power**
**stability and order**
**eternity and continuity**
**restrained strength**
**material and cultural value**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Power become an institution; dignity become a form of sovereign grandeur.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human is the central element of the style.

The human appears as:

* the head of an institution or dynasty
* a judge, academician or executive
* a keeper of imperial tradition
* part of a dynastic line (portraits of predecessors)
* a figure presented in a study or a hall

Preferred:

* restraint and dignity
* stillness and stability
* frontal staging
* an absence of pathos and posturing
* faces that carry will, experience and power

The human figure must not turn into a caricatured monarch or a symbol of despotism. It is a bearer of institutional grandeur.

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-11 is presented **with restraint and functionality**:

* laptops, tablets, smartphones as instruments of power and governance
* Chronicle OS digital interfaces, styled after imperial documents
* DOI marks, verification keys, cryptographic signatures
* architectural models and digital blueprints

Technology must not dominate the imperial layer. It is an **instrument of institutional control**, not a theme in its own right.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-11 **is not**:

* a literal reconstruction of an imperial ceremonial portrait
* a trite Neoclassical styling
* fantasy aristocracy
* steampunk with an excess of mechanisms
* cheap stock clip art
* a chaotic collage of imperial symbols
* a caricature of monarchy

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If the imperial image and the institutional environment exist separately from one another, or degenerate into a set of clip art, the work does not conform to I-11.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**witness**
the element carries the memory of imperial tradition;

**symbolic**
the element conveys status, grandeur, institutional meaning;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and holds the gaze.

Elements that exist for the sake of visual noise or meaningless accumulation of accessories are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-11 permits a range from photorealistic oil painting to academic imperial illustration.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**material credibility (oil, wool, gold, wood)**
**imperial blue and gold as the basis of the palette**
**strict ceremonial symmetry**
**a symbolic layer of grandeur, power and institutional continuity**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* certificates of authenticity
* sovereign mandates
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* imperial monograph covers
* frontispieces
* illustrations for corporate publications
* collector's editions

**Visual identity**

* the design of the IMARCH Bonus Boutique
* presentation materials
* premium cases
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* sovereign collector series
* corporate galleries

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-11 if it contains all of the following:

**1. A monumental human figure in imperial attire or a strict suit.**

**2. A material basis of oil, wool, gold and wood.**

**3. Imperial blue and gold as the basis of the palette.**

**4. Strict ceremonial symmetry and a study environment.**

**5. A sense of imperial grandeur, sovereign dignity and institutional power.**

If only an imperial accessory appears, without a person, it is not I-11.

If only a portrait appears, without the institutional environment, it is not I-11.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **A monumental imperial portrait in the technique of oil painting: a person in an imperial blue suit or mantle, gold accessories, watch, quill, a strict study with oak panelling, portraits of predecessors in gilded frames, bookcases, globes, architectural models; directed light, deep shadow in the background, strict central symmetry, and a sense of imperial grandeur, sovereign dignity, institutional power and the eternal continuity of IMARCH.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-11 does not depict power for power's sake.**

**I-11 depicts imperial grandeur become an institution, and sovereign dignity become a form of governance.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

All three models described below belong to the umbrella category of **Collector & Commercial Rights**, as recorded in the archive manifesto **RN-0044-GD-0001** (section 8, subsection 1). This category is the common legal field of the series: it describes the full scope of rights that may be transferred at all, while each specific model defines the scope of rights the purchaser receives.

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
