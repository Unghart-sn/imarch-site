#### 1. I-12 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-12. Digital Minimalism

#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-12
**Series:** I
**Name:** Digital Minimalism
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**A sterile laboratory environment + a cold blue palette + glossy surfaces + biomechanical components + digital interfaces + IMARCH institutional precision.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of a scientific laboratory nor a trite sci-fi styling, but a visual language in its own right, in which purity, precision, technological control and a sovereign future exist as elements of a single institutional aesthetic.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-12:

> **Precision is not a technical characteristic, but the visual form of sovereign institutional control over matter and data.**

The human figure and the biomechanical object are perceived as bearers of purity, sterility and high-tech precision.

The cold blue palette and glossy surfaces represent technological perfection and control.

Digital interfaces join the material and the informational into a single system of sovereign knowledge.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**purity / complexity**
**precision / chaos**
**gloss / matter**
**control / discovery**
**digital / biological**
**future / tradition**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials:

**Glossy white plastic**
Polished surfaces, sterile forms, medical precision.

**Chrome-plated metal**
Polished steel, mirror surfaces, joint articulations.

**Cold blue light**
LED panels, illumination, digital interfaces.

**Transparent glass**
Screens, flasks, lenses, laboratory optics.

**Biomechanical components**
Cables, hinges, joint articulations, implants, neural interfaces.

**Digital interfaces**
Transparent panels, holographic schematics, DNA models, medical data.

Materials must look physically convincing: real gloss, real steel, real light, not flat vector graphics.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* biomechanical heads and faces (closed eyes)
* robotic hands and surgical instruments
* neural interfaces and neural networks
* DNA helices and molecular models
* medical scanners and digital schematics of the body
* holographic panels and interfaces
* laboratory capsules and operating tables
* manipulators and industrial robots
* sterile operating and laboratory environments
* blue LED panels and illumination
* IMARCH institutional markers

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and institutional attribution, not as random decorative props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Sterile geometry**

Objects are constructed on strict geometric canons: clean lines, polished surfaces, precise angles.

**Biomechanical symmetry**

The central object (head, face, body) is built on the principle of symmetry and functional precision.

**Glossy materiality**

Surfaces reflect light, creating the impression of a studio render and sterile purity.

**Digital layering**

The composition is built on the principle of layering: foreground — the operation or the object, mid-ground — the interfaces, background — the laboratory environment.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of laboratory hierarchy:

**principal object (head, face, body, biomechanism) → operation or instrument → digital interfaces → laboratory environment.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central axis
* frontal or profile staging
* high visual stability
* controlled depth of space
* cold directed light highlighting the principal object
* a limited number of secondary details
* contrast between a lit object and a cold background

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* cold blue (cyan, light blue, azure)
* sterile white
* polished silver

**Secondary palette:**

* dark blue and graphite
* transparent crystal
* cold violet (for accents)

**Light accents:**

* a cold LED glow
* blue highlights on gloss and metal
* localized glow of interfaces and sensors

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with cold blue, white and silver.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is a structural element shaping sterile precision.

Preferred:

* cold directed light
* blue LED panels and illumination
* mirror reflections on glossy surfaces
* contrast between a lit object and a cold background

Light must emphasize the precision and purity of the object, not its drama.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-12 must create a sense of:

**sterile purity**
**high-tech precision**
**institutional control**
**a sovereign future**
**digital perfection**
**biomechanical harmony**
**calm concentration**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Precision become an institution; technology become a form of sovereign control.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human in I-12 is present **as an operator, researcher or biomechanical object**:

* a biomechanical head with closed eyes
* human hands in gloves, working with an instrument
* a researcher in a sterile laboratory suit
* an operator working with digital interfaces
* a figure dissolved into a neural interface

The human figure retains the traits of restrained concentration, professional precision and well-bred composure. It must not turn into a superhero or a victim — it is the operator of the system.

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-12 is presented **as an advanced engineering system**:

* biomechanical components and implants
* neural interfaces and digital schematics of the brain
* surgical manipulators and robotic hands
* laboratory scanners and capsules
* DNA models and molecular structures
* DOI marks, verification keys, cryptographic signatures
* Chronicle OS digital interfaces, styled after laboratory terminals

Technology must visually merge with the biomechanical object, forming a single whole.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-12 **is not**:

* a trite sci-fi laboratory
* a neon disco
* horror medicine
* a fantasy cyborg
* steampunk
* a pure medical textbook
* cheap 3D clip art
* a chaotic collage of laboratory equipment

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If the biomechanical object and the technological environment exist separately from one another, or degenerate into a set of clip art, the work does not conform to I-12.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**structural**
the element explains the construction of the system or the laboratory;

**symbolic**
the element conveys precision, purity, institutional meaning;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and holds the gaze.

Elements that exist for the sake of visual noise or meaningless accumulation of equipment are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-12 permits a range from photorealistic 3D rendering to stylized conceptual illustration.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**a cold blue palette**
**glossy materiality and sterile purity**
**a biomechanical or digital central object**
**a sense of precision, control and a sovereign future**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* certificates of authenticity
* sovereign mandates
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* technological monograph covers
* frontispieces
* illustrations for scientific research
* collector's editions

**Visual identity**

* the design of the IMARCH Bonus Boutique
* presentation materials
* premium cases
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* sovereign collector series
* musical visual concepts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-12 if it contains all of the following:

**1. A biomechanical or digital central object (head, face, body, implant).**

**2. A sterile laboratory environment or digital interfaces.**

**3. A cold blue palette and glossy surfaces.**

**4. An operator or researcher as an element of the system.**

**5. A sense of precision, purity, control and a sovereign future.**

If only a blue laboratory appears, without a biomechanical object, it is not I-12.

If only a biomechanical object appears, without the laboratory environment, it is not I-12.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **A sterile high-tech laboratory: a biomechanical head with closed eyes, chrome-plated metal, glossy white plastic, cold blue LED light, transparent digital interfaces, neural interfaces and DNA models, surgical manipulators, robotic hands, laboratory capsules; cold directed light, mirror reflections, a strict central composition, and a sense of sterile purity, high-tech precision, institutional control and the sovereign future of IMARCH.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-12 does not depict technology for technology's sake.**

**I-12 depicts precision become an institution, and control become a form of the sovereign future.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

All three models described below belong to the umbrella category of **Collector & Commercial Rights**, as recorded in the archive manifesto **RN-0044-GD-0001** (section 8, subsection 1). This category is the common legal field of the series: it describes the full scope of rights that may be transferred at all, while each specific model defines the scope of rights the purchaser receives.

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
