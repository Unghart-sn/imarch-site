## I. PREAMBLE

Founding Institutional Declaration of the International Institutional Registry VERDICTUM.

This Declaration establishes the institutional, methodological and organisational foundations of the International Institutional Registry VERDICTUM.

VERDICTUM is established as a foundational institution within the Public Knowledge Infrastructure (PKI), providing a structured environment for the registration, verification, preservation and institutional use of documented public knowledge.

This Declaration defines the purpose, institutional principles, governance model and architectural foundations upon which VERDICTUM shall operate and develop.

The provisions of this Declaration constitute the highest level of the internal normative framework governing the Registry and serve as the reference foundation for all subsequent architectural, methodological, editorial and technological specifications.

&nbsp;

## II. INSTITUTIONAL STATUS OF VERDICTUM

VERDICTUM is established as an independent institutional registry operating within the Public Knowledge Infrastructure (PKI).

The Registry is not constituted as a news outlet, a scientific periodical, a social media platform, a crowdfunding service, or a commercial information system.

Its institutional purpose is fundamentally distinct from the functions traditionally performed by such entities.

VERDICTUM serves as a permanent institutional environment for the registration, verification, preservation and structured use of documented events, evidence, decisions and institutional records.

The Registry performs the function of institutional memory by maintaining a continuously evolving system of documented objects whose integrity, traceability and evidential value are preserved throughout their entire life cycle.

Accordingly, VERDICTUM shall be regarded as an institutional infrastructure rather than as a publishing platform.

Its primary function is not the dissemination of information but the establishment of verifiable institutional knowledge capable of supporting responsible decision making across public, scientific, governmental and commercial domains.

&nbsp;

## III. MISSION AND PUBLIC PURPOSE

The mission of VERDICTUM is to establish a trusted institutional environment in which documented public knowledge may be created, preserved, verified and utilised as a reliable foundation for responsible decision making.

VERDICTUM recognises that the long-term stability of public institutions depends not solely upon the availability of information but upon the existence of documented evidence whose provenance, integrity and institutional context remain verifiable over time.

Accordingly, the Registry is designed to reduce uncertainty in public, scientific, commercial and civic interactions by providing an institutional mechanism through which documented events acquire continuity, traceability and evidential permanence.

VERDICTUM does not seek to replace existing information systems, scientific publications, governmental registers or commercial databases.

Its purpose is to complement such systems by establishing a common evidential layer through which independently documented facts, institutional commitments and verified outcomes may be connected into a coherent and continuously developing body of institutional memory.

The public value of VERDICTUM therefore lies not in the production of additional information, but in increasing the reliability, accountability and long-term usability of documented knowledge within the Public Knowledge Infrastructure.

&nbsp;

## IV. FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLES

The institutional operation of VERDICTUM is governed by a coherent system of foundational principles that determine the creation, verification, preservation and institutional use of documented knowledge throughout the Public Knowledge Infrastructure.

These principles constitute the normative framework within which all organisational, methodological and technological components of the Registry shall operate.

No architectural implementation, software solution or administrative procedure may contradict the principles established by this Declaration.

### 4.1. Principle of Institutional Integrity

Every documented object shall preserve its institutional identity throughout its entire life cycle.

Its provenance, evidential basis, institutional context and documented relationships shall remain traceable irrespective of subsequent technological, organisational or procedural transformations.

Institutional integrity constitutes a mandatory condition for the recognition of any object within VERDICTUM.

### 4.2. Principle of Verifiability

Every institutional statement, documented event or recorded decision shall possess a verifiable evidential basis.

Verification shall rely upon documented sources, transparent provenance and reproducible institutional procedures.

Assertions that cannot be independently verified shall not acquire institutional status within the Registry.

### 4.3. Principle of Accountability

Institutional responsibility accompanies every documented action.

The Registry preserves not only documented outcomes but also the institutional accountability associated with their creation, verification and subsequent use.

Responsibility therefore remains inseparable from documented institutional activity.

### 4.4. Principle of Institutional Continuity

Documented knowledge shall be regarded as a continuously developing institutional process rather than as a collection of isolated records.

Every newly documented object may extend, refine or contextualise previously established institutional knowledge while preserving the continuity of its evidential history.

&nbsp;

## V. ARCHITECTURE OF INSTITUTIONAL INTERACTION

VERDICTUM operates through a structured model of institutional interaction in which documented actions, rather than individual participants, constitute the primary objects of institutional processing.

Participants initiate institutional processes by performing documented actions within their respective institutional roles.

These actions give rise to documented objects that subsequently become part of the continuously developing institutional memory maintained by the Registry.

Accordingly, institutional interaction within VERDICTUM shall not be understood as communication between users, but as the coordinated processing of documented institutional actions through a common evidential framework.

### 5.1. Institutional Action as the Primary Processing Unit

Within VERDICTUM, institutional action constitutes the primary operational unit.

The Registry neither evaluates personal identity nor processes participants as isolated entities.

Institutional processing is initiated exclusively by documented actions that produce legally and organisationally significant consequences.

Each documented action creates or modifies institutional objects in accordance with the principles established by this Declaration.

&nbsp;

## VI. LIFE CYCLE OF A VERDICTUM RECORD (VR)

Every Verdictum Record (VR) is regarded as an institutional object possessing a defined life cycle.

The institutional value of a VR derives not solely from the information it contains, but from the documented sequence of actions through which the object is created, verified, maintained and subsequently utilised within the Public Knowledge Infrastructure.

Accordingly, a Verdictum Record shall not be understood as a static publication.

It constitutes a continuously evolving institutional object whose evidential significance may develop over time while preserving the integrity of its documented history.

### 6.1. Creation

The life cycle of a Verdictum Record begins with the emergence of a documented institutional action.

It is such an action that creates the legal and organisational basis for the formation of a new institutional object.

At the moment of creation, the Registry records the primary identity of the VR, its documented provenance, institutional context and initial evidential basis.

### 6.2. Verification

Following its creation, every Verdictum Record becomes subject to institutional verification.

Verification confirms the authenticity of documented sources, evaluates the evidential basis of the recorded action and establishes the institutional status of the object.

The degree of verification forms an integral component of the institutional identity of the VR throughout its subsequent existence.

### 6.3. Development

Institutional objects may acquire additional documented evidence, analytical assessments, legal references and verified relationships with other Verdictum Records.

Such development extends the institutional significance of the object without altering its documented provenance or compromising the continuity of its evidential history.

Institutional development shall therefore be regarded as cumulative rather than substitutive.

### 6.4. Institutional Use

Verified Verdictum Records may subsequently serve as evidential references for institutional decision making, scientific research, governance, investment assessment, legal documentation and other activities requiring documented institutional reliability.

Institutional use does not alter the identity of the Record.

Rather, it extends the practical value of documented knowledge across multiple institutional contexts.

### 6.5. Preservation

The completion of individual institutional processes shall not terminate the existence of a Verdictum Record.

Every VR remains part of the institutional memory of the Public Knowledge Infrastructure and continues to preserve its documented relationships, evidential history and institutional significance for future reference.

Institutional memory is therefore regarded as an accumulating public asset rather than a temporary archive.

&nbsp;

## VII. VERDICTUM RECORD AS AN INSTITUTIONAL OBJECT

Within VERDICTUM, the Verdictum Record (VR) constitutes the primary institutional object through which documented knowledge is created, organised, verified and preserved.

A Verdictum Record shall not be interpreted as a conventional document, publication or database entry.

Rather, it represents an autonomous institutional entity possessing a persistent identity, a documented provenance, an evidential status, institutional relationships and a continuously evolving operational history.

Accordingly, the institutional significance of a VR extends beyond the informational content contained within a single record.

Its value derives from the structured system of documented relationships established throughout its institutional life cycle.

&nbsp;

## VIII. POSITION OF VERDICTUM WITHIN THE PUBLIC KNOWLEDGE INFRASTRUCTURE

VERDICTUM constitutes one of the core institutional components of the Public Knowledge Infrastructure (PKI).

Its function is to preserve institutional memory through the registration, verification and long-term maintenance of documented institutional objects.

Within the Public Knowledge Infrastructure, VERDICTUM performs functions that are complementary to, but distinct from, systems responsible for scientific research, software development, governance, education and institutional management.

Accordingly, the Registry neither replaces nor duplicates existing institutional mechanisms.

Instead, it establishes the evidential continuity through which independently operating institutional systems may exchange documented knowledge within a common framework of trust.

### 8.1. Relationship with Chronicle OS

Within the IMARCH ecosystem, Chronicle OS and VERDICTUM constitute complementary institutional environments.

Chronicle OS is responsible for the creation, systematisation and dissemination of reproducible public knowledge.

VERDICTUM is responsible for documenting institutional reality through verified records, evidential chains and documented outcomes.

Scientific knowledge generated within Chronicle OS may subsequently acquire institutional implementation through VERDICTUM.

Conversely, documented institutional experience accumulated within VERDICTUM may initiate new scientific research, methodological development and technological innovation within Chronicle OS.

Together, both systems establish a continuous institutional cycle connecting knowledge generation with institutional practice.

&nbsp;

## IX. GOVERNANCE AND INSTITUTIONAL EVOLUTION

The Public Knowledge Infrastructure is designed as an evolving institutional system.

Accordingly, VERDICTUM shall develop through documented institutional processes while preserving the continuity of its foundational principles.

Institutional evolution shall be understood as the controlled extension of organisational, methodological and technological capabilities without compromising institutional integrity, evidential continuity or public accountability.

Changes affecting the Registry shall themselves constitute documented institutional actions and shall therefore become part of the institutional memory maintained by VERDICTUM.

Institutional governance is consequently inseparable from institutional documentation.

The evolution of the Registry is itself a documented institutional process.

### 9.1. Principle of Self-Documentation

Every institutional modification affecting the architecture, governance or methodology of VERDICTUM shall itself be documented within the institutional environment established by this Declaration.

Accordingly, the Registry remains capable of preserving not only documented external events but also the documented history of its own institutional evolution.

Institutional transparency therefore extends to the governance of the Registry itself.

&nbsp;

## X. FOUNDING PROVISION

This Declaration establishes the institutional foundations of VERDICTUM as an integral component of the Public Knowledge Infrastructure.

The principles, definitions and institutional relationships set forth herein shall constitute the conceptual framework governing the further organisational, methodological, technological and legal development of the Registry.

Future regulations, specifications, governance documents, software architectures and operational procedures shall derive from, and remain consistent with, the institutional principles established by this Declaration.

Accordingly, this Declaration shall be regarded as the primary constitutional document defining the institutional identity, public purpose and developmental continuity of VERDICTUM.

Its purpose is not merely to describe an institution, but to establish the normative architecture within which that institution may continuously evolve while preserving its identity.

&nbsp;

## XI. FINAL PROVISION

The institutional principles established by this Declaration are intended to provide a stable conceptual framework for the continued development of VERDICTUM and the broader Public Knowledge Infrastructure.

The Declaration shall serve as the common point of reference for institutional governance, methodological development, software architecture, legal documentation and organisational practice within the Registry.

The continued evolution of VERDICTUM shall preserve the continuity of these principles while allowing the institutional system to expand through documented, verifiable and publicly accountable processes.

Accordingly, the identity of VERDICTUM shall be determined not by individual technologies, software implementations or organisational forms, but by the institutional principles expressed in this Declaration.

Technological solutions may evolve.

Administrative structures may evolve.

Legal instruments may evolve.

Operational procedures may evolve.

The institutional identity shall remain continuous.

For this reason, the present Declaration shall remain the foundational reference document of VERDICTUM unless amended through the institutional procedures established by the Registry itself.
