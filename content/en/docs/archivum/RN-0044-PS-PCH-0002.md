#### 1. I-2 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-2. Antiquarian Bonus Boutique (The Researcher's Study / Elite Heritage)

<a id="section-1"></a>
#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-2
**Series:** I
**Name:** Antiquarian Bonus Boutique (The Researcher's Study / Elite Heritage)
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**Nineteenth-century researcher's study + brass mechanisms + warm candlelight + old leather bindings + sealing wax + IMARCH institutional artefacts.**

The style is neither a literal museum reconstruction of a historical interior nor a trite vintage styling, but a sovereign visual language in its own right, in which age-old academic traditions, the material value of antiquities and the closed technological circuits of the ecosystem exist as elements of a single civilizational system.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-2:

> **Knowledge and technology carry weight, history and mystery, safeguarded by the traditions of closed studies and academic scholarship.**

Ancient and classical forms are perceived as bearers of memory, continuity and trust.

Brass mechanisms and institutional elements represent technological development, precision of calculation and sovereign control.

The environment joins both strata into a single system of elite presence.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**age-old tradition / modern sovereign system**
**candlelight / researcher's lamp**
**handwritten folio / digital registry**
**secrecy / impeccable authenticity**
**the matter of the past / the digital capital of the future**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials:

**Dark wood (walnut, oak)**
The texture of polished wood in writing desks, panels and caskets.

**Brass and bronze**
The material of candlesticks, astrolabes, spectacle frames, magnifying glasses and chronometer mechanisms.

**Natural leather**
Heavy embossed folio bindings, furniture upholstery, the green baize of judicial and study desks.

**Sealing wax and parchment**
The dense texture of paper with wax seals and official charters.

**Gilded embossed ornament**
Elements of IMARCH heraldry and branding on book spines, wallets and textiles.

Materials must look physically convincing: preference is given to a sense of mass, noble patina, traces of wear and the structural logic of genuine objects.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* astronomical instruments, globes with glowing maps
* valve radios and retro interfaces in wooden casings
* hourglasses, pocket chronometers and stopwatches
* quill pens, inkwells, stacks of gold coins
* scrolls with sealing wax and antique folios
* branded elements of the ecosystem (gold-embroidered polo shirts, digital graphs shown at stylized angles)

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and the attribution of an elite space, not as random decorative props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Tactile density**

Objects must possess visual weight, textural depth and a sense of material value.

**Study-like order**

The composition is built around the researcher's central workplace or repository of artefacts.

**Historical accuracy of geometry**

The proportions of furniture, instruments and architectural elements follow the canons of classical studies and libraries.

**Institutional modularity**

Elements of the ecosystem are integrated into the antiquarian environment without stylistic rupture.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of still-life and spatial hierarchy:

**principal measuring or symbolic object (clock, globe, astrolabe) → folios, scrolls and working tools → background environment (bookcases, maps, panels) → accent lighting details.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central or diagonal perspective
* high visual stability
* controlled depth of space, with soft fading into shadow
* directed light highlighting the key nodes of the composition
* a limited and carefully judged number of secondary details

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* warm tones: ochre, amber light, gold, terracotta
* deep tones: dark walnut, green baize, burgundy velvet, deep indigo

**Light accents:**

* the soft flicker of candle flames
* localized glow of lamps with green/brass shades
* focused glow of astronomical instruments and displays in retro casings

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with noble wood, metal and leather.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is the key structural element in creating the atmosphere.

Preferred:

* soft directed light from desk lamps
* living, flickering candle flames with physically accurate interplay of shadows
* golden reflections and highlights on polished brass and book spines
* deep, velvety shadows in the corners of the study

Light must emphasize volume, the texture of materials and the meaningful hierarchy of the composition.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-2 must create a sense of:

**intellectual authority**
**age-old continuity**
**absolute confidentiality**
**academic power**
**the material and cultural value of artefacts**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Knowledge protected by tradition, by time and by the sovereign circuit.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human may appear as:

* a keeper of the archive
* a researcher-analyst
* an operator of the sovereign circuit
* the owner of the bonus boutique
* a scale element of the study environment

The human figure retains the traits of restrained dignity, concentration and well-bred composure. Clothing and accessories are harmoniously attuned to the period (classical elements of dress, IMARCH branded textiles).

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-2 appears as the apex of mechanical and early electronic development:

* precision brass gears and chronometer dials
* valve amplifiers and indicators in polished wooden casings
* encrypted Chronicle OS digital interfaces, styled after analogue instruments and light panels

Modern technologies visually merge with the antiquarian world, creating the impression of the researcher's timeless toolkit.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-2 **is not**:

* a trite reconstruction of a historical interior
* an antique shop filled with clutter
* a museum display without modern context
* steampunk with an excess of gears
* a fantasy wizard's study
* cheap retro kitsch

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If the historical antiquities and the modern elements of the ecosystem exist separately from one another, or degenerate into a caricatured collection of props, the work does not conform to I-2.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**structural**
the element explains the construction of the instrument, the furniture or the system;

**symbolic**
the element conveys status, historical or institutional meaning;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and directs the viewer's eye.

Elements that exist purely for visual noise or meaningless accumulation of objects are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-2 permits a range from photorealistic depiction of a study environment to classical artistic illustration.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**material credibility (wood, leather, brass)**
**a warm, intimate lighting scenario**
**the presence of IMARCH institutional markers**
**a strict still-life or spatial composition**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* certificates of authenticity
* sovereign mandates
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* collector's monograph covers
* frontispieces
* illustrations for archival reports
* collector's editions

**Visual identity**

* the design of the IMARCH Bonus Boutique
* presentation materials
* premium cases
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* sovereign collector series
* musical visual concepts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-2 if it contains all of the following:

**1. The material basis of a classical study (polished wood, leather, metal).**

**2. A pronounced warm-light scenario (lamps, candles, soft shadows).**

**3. Institutional or technological elements of the IMARCH ecosystem.**

**4. A stable, balanced composition.**

**5. A sense of historical depth, weight and the value of the artefact.**

If only incidental antiquities appear, unconnected to the ecosystem, it is not I-2.

If pure modern high-tech appears, without the warm study basis, it is not I-2.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **A nineteenth-century researcher's study, polished dark wood, patinated brass, bronze, old leather bindings, sealing wax, astrolabes, globes, valve instruments, the warm light of candles and desk lamps with green shades; a strict still-life composition, high tactile density of materials, and a sense of eternity, intellectual mystery and the material value of sovereign IMARCH assets.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-2 does not imitate the past for the sake of decoration.**

**I-2 affirms the continuity of an intellectual elite, in which age-old traditions of enquiry and the modern sovereign circuit form a single, indivisible repository of value.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

All three models described below belong to the umbrella category of **Collector & Commercial Rights**, as recorded in the archive manifesto **RN-0044-GD-0001** (section 8, subsection 1). This category is the common legal field of the series: it describes the full scope of rights that may be transferred at all, while each specific model defines the scope of rights the purchaser receives.

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
