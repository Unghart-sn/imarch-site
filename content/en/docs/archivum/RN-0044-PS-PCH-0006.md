#### 1. I-6 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-6. Formal Ceremonial Memorial Style

#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-6
**Series:** I
**Name:** Formal Ceremonial Memorial Style
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**Engraving + bronze relief + pencil drawing + laurel wreaths + memorial plaque + IMARCH institutional portrait.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of a memorial plaque nor a trite ceremonial illustration, but a visual language in its own right, in which significance, respect, memory and honour exist as elements of a single institutional tradition.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-6:

> **Memory is not the past, but an active institutional force fixed in material and form.**

The human figure is perceived as a bearer of dignity, merit and institutional memory.

Bronze, marble and engraving represent material eternity and official recognition.

IMARCH institutional artefacts (memorial plaques, portraits, charters) join personal memory to the sovereign registry.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**memory / oblivion**
**bronze / paper**
**engraving / photograph**
**official / personal**
**eternity / moment**
**honour / indifference**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials:

**Bronze**
Patinated metal, relief portraits, memorial plaques, laurel branches.

**Marble**
Polished stone, pedestals, frames, architectural elements.

**Engraving**
Fine hatching technique, linear grid, banknote-level detail.

**Pencil drawing**
Soft shading, graphite texture, academic portrait.

**Paper and parchment**
The dense texture of old documents, charters, testimonials.

**Gold and brass**
Frames, ornaments, plaques, engraving.

Materials must look physically convincing: real bronze, real engraving, real pencil, not a digital filter.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* laurel wreaths and branches
* memorial plaques with inscriptions
* bronze relief portraits
* engraved portraits in an oval or circle
* academic pencil portraits
* mosaic panels and ancient ornaments
* the inscriptions IN MEMORIAM, MEMORIA, HONOR
* Latin formulae and mottoes
* plaques with names and dates
* IMARCH institutional emblems
* architectural frames and portals
* medallions and seals

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and memorial attribution, not as random decorative props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Monumentality**

Objects possess visual weight, stability and a sense of durability.

**Portrait hierarchy**

The central figure — a face, bust or relief — organizes the space around itself.

**Official symmetry**

The composition follows the canons of the ceremonial portrait: central axis, stable proportions, clear frames.

**Material hierarchy**

The principal object — a portrait or memorial plaque — is subordinated to a single compositional logic.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of memorial hierarchy:

**principal portrait or relief → memorial inscription → ornamental frame → background environment (mosaic, bronze, stone).**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central axis
* frontal staging
* high visual stability
* controlled depth of space
* directed light highlighting the face
* a limited number of secondary details
* a background that works as context, not as a separate scene

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* warm tones: bronze, ochre, sepia, amber
* neutral tones: marble white, grey stone, graphite

**Secondary palette:**

* deep green (patina, laurel)
* burgundy and dark red (seals, fabric)
* dark blue (mosaic, background)

**Light accents:**

* a warm bronze glow
* soft light on the face and on marble
* localized highlights on metal and engraving

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with bronze, stone and muted warm tones.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is a bearer of meaning: it separates the eternal from the transient.

Preferred:

* soft directed light highlighting the face
* bronze reflections on patinated metal
* contrast between a lit portrait and a shadowed background
* a background glow on marble and engraving

Light must emphasize the dignity of the figure, not its drama.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-6 must create a sense of:

**significance**
**respect**
**memory**
**honour**
**institutional dignity**
**eternal continuity**
**material and cultural value**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Memory become an institution; dignity become material.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human is the central element of the style.

The human may appear as:

* a bronze relief portrait
* an engraved portrait in an oval
* an academic pencil portrait
* a mosaic image
* a figure in formal dress

Preferred:

* restraint and dignity
* stillness and stability
* frontal or slightly turned staging
* an absence of pathos and posturing
* faces that carry memory but are not broken

The human figure must not automatically become a lone hero or a symbol of victimhood. It is a witness and a bearer of institutional memory.

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-6 is presented **in moderation and in an institutional register**:

* engraved grids and banknote textures
* DOI marks, verification keys, cryptographic signatures
* Chronicle OS digital interfaces, styled after memorial tablets
* glowing elements as a metaphor for eternal memory

Technology must not dominate the memorial layer. It is an **instrument for recording memory**, not a theme in its own right.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-6 **is not**:

* a literal reconstruction of a memorial plaque
* a museum display without modern context
* a trite ceremonial portrait
* a fantasy emblem
* steampunk with an excess of mechanisms
* cheap 3D clip art
* a chaotic collage of memorial symbols

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If the memorial symbol and the institutional system exist separately from one another, or degenerate into a set of clip art, the work does not conform to I-6.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**witness**
the element carries the memory of a person or an event;

**symbolic**
the element conveys status, honour, institutional meaning;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and holds the gaze.

Elements that exist for the sake of visual noise or meaningless accumulation of symbols are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-6 permits a range from photorealistic bronze to academic engraving.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**material credibility (bronze, marble, engraving, graphite)**
**a warm, muted lighting scenario**
**official symmetry of composition**
**a symbolic layer of memory and institutional honour**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* testimonials of memory
* certificates of authenticity
* sovereign mandates
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* memorial monograph covers
* frontispieces
* illustrations for archival research
* collector's editions

**Visual identity**

* the design of the IMARCH Bonus Boutique
* presentation materials
* premium cases
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* sovereign collector series
* musical visual concepts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-6 if it contains all of the following:

**1. A memorial or portrait central object (bust, relief, engraving, portrait).**

**2. A material basis of bronze, marble, engraving or graphite.**

**3. Official symmetry and ceremonial composition.**

**4. A memorial inscription or institutional symbol.**

**5. A sense of significance, respect, memory and institutional honour.**

If only memorial clip art appears, without a system, it is not I-6.

If a pure portrait appears, without institutional context, it is not I-6.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **A formal ceremonial memorial portrait: a bronze relief or an engraved portrait in an oval, a marble frame, laurel wreaths, a memorial plaque inscribed IN MEMORIAM, patinated metal, academic pencil drawing, mosaic panels, warm muted light, a strict central composition, and a sense of significance, respect, memory and the institutional honour of IMARCH.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-6 does not depict memory for the sake of decoration.**

**I-6 depicts memory become an institution, and dignity become material.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

All three models described below belong to the umbrella category of **Collector & Commercial Rights**, as recorded in the archive manifesto **RN-0044-GD-0001** (section 8, subsection 1). This category is the common legal field of the series: it describes the full scope of rights that may be transferred at all, while each specific model defines the scope of rights the purchaser receives.

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
