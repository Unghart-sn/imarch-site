#### Why Timestamp Registration Matters { .fade-in-up }

In professional and creative work, it is often important to establish that particular material existed in a specific form before publication, disclosure to third parties, the commencement of negotiations, subsequent revision or another significant event.

A Timestamp Certificate provides a verifiable record associated with a digital object and the time of its registration.

{{< ts-panel >}}
It may be used to register:

* manuscripts, texts, concepts and research materials;
* visual artworks, photographs, designs and musical compositions;
* software code, technical drawings, architectural solutions and specifications;
* calculations, analytical reports, project documentation and intermediate results;
* versions of proposals, working materials and documents relating to a particular stage of a project.
{{< /ts-panel >}}

The purpose of the procedure is to create a verifiable chronological reference that may subsequently be used for version comparison, chronology assessment and the presentation of documentary material.

#### Verification and Documentary Use { .fade-in-up }

The Timestamp Certificate is intended to substantiate registration details and verify whether a digital object corresponds to its registered state.

{{< ts-panel >}}
It may be used as supplementary documentary material for:

* comparing different versions of a digital object;
* documenting the sequence of development or creation of material;
* recording intermediate results;
* presenting material to partners, clients, experts or research organisations;
* preparing supporting evidence in a particular legal or professional context.
{{< /ts-panel >}}

#### Frequently Asked Questions { .fade-in-up }

{{< details "What does a Timestamp Certificate establish?" >}}
It provides a verifiable record associated with a digital object and a recorded timestamp. Subject to the technical properties and validity of the registration procedure, it may help establish that the corresponding digital data existed in a particular form no later than the recorded time.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "Does the certificate prove authorship or ownership?" >}}
No. A Timestamp Certificate does not independently establish authorship, ownership, originality, intellectual property rights or the legal validity of a document. It may provide supporting evidence in conjunction with other records and evidence.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "Does IMARCH store my original file?" >}}
The original file is processed to generate its cryptographic hash. It is not retained within the infrastructure after the registration procedure has been completed.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "Can I register confidential material?" >}}
The standard registration procedure does not retain the original file after completion. Users handling highly confidential material may also consider the Hash-Only Registration option, where available, which allows registration of a pre-generated cryptographic hash without uploading the original file.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "Can the certificate be used in legal proceedings?" >}}
It may be presented as supporting evidence, subject to the applicable law, the nature of the dispute and the assessment of the relevant authority. The certificate is not a substitute for legal advice, expert examination or other evidence.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "Can I give a certificate to someone else?" >}}
Yes. A Gift Certificate is issued with a unique activation code that may be transferred to another person. The recipient activates the code through the website and uses the registration entitlement for their own digital object.
{{< /details >}}

{{< details "How many registrations are included in the Professional Pack?" >}}
The Professional Pack includes five independent registrations for £90. Each registration may be used for a separate digital object or version.
{{< /details >}}
