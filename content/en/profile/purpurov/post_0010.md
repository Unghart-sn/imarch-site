#### 🎙️ The Foundation of a Project's Form Stability { .fade-in-up }

**The founder of a humanitarian project always faces the challenge: how to turn a personal idea into collective action.**

The founder of a humanitarian project always faces the challenge: how to turn a personal idea into collective action. In the early stages, it seems that enthusiasm and belief are enough. But it is mentoring that helps to travel the path from inspiration to a sustainable form.

A mentor is not just an advisor, but a guide who helps to identify weak points, adjust the strategy and turn the concept into a working system. In the humanitarian sphere this is especially important: here value is measured not by profit but by social impact, and therefore mistakes are costlier.

Official support programmes, experienced mentors and institutional practices become that very “social capital” that allows a project to survive and develop. Mentoring is a test of an idea’s viability, its adaptation to reality and its transformation into an instrument of real change.
