#### 1. VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT I-1

#### 1.1. Style Identification

**Code:** I-1  
**Series:** I  
**Title:** Futuristic Ancient Egyptian Cyberpunk  
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic-constructive style

**Base Formula:**

**Sacred Architecture of Ancient Egypt + Biomechanics + Futuristic Technology + Monumental Symmetry.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of Ancient Egyptian culture nor a conventional sci-fi stylization, but an independent visual language in which archaic sacred architecture and high-technology biomechanical environments coexist as elements of a single civilizational system.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual Core

Core principle of I-1:

> **The past and the future are presented not as opposites, but as successive technological layers of a single civilization.**

Ancient forms are perceived as bearers of memory, order, and sacred knowledge.

Mechanical elements represent technological development, functionality, and the transformation of the human body.

The futuristic environment unites both layers into a unified architectural system.

Primary semantic oppositions:

**eternity / technology**  
**ritual / function**  
**stone / metal**  
**human / mechanism**  
**antiquity / future**  
**sacred / engineering**

---

#### 1.3. Material Code

Primary materials:

**Lapis Lazuli**  
A deep blue material used as the primary sacred and color marker.

**Gold**  
A symbol of durability, status, light, and technological value.

**White Stone**  
The architectural foundation, creating a sense of monumentality and antiquity.

**Bronze and Brass**  
Transitional materials between ancient craftsmanship and mechanical construction.

**Dark Metal**  
Functional mechanical elements, internal structures, joints, and technological details.

**Biomechanical Components**  
Articulations, hinges, cabling structures, mechanical implants, protective elements, and functional connections.

Materials must appear physically convincing. Preference is given to the sensation of mass, surface texture, wear, and constructive logic.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic Code

Permissible motifs:

* temple architecture
* columns
* pediments
* obelisks
* sacred geometric ornaments
* solar and celestial motifs
* hieroglyphic graphics
* stylized Ancient Egyptian symbols
* anthropomorphic and divine forms
* biomechanical joints
* mechanical implants
* technological interfaces
* structural metal connections

Symbols must be used as part of the composition and architectural system, rather than as a random decorative set.

When utilizing Anunnaki imagery, they are treated as an **artistic mythological motif** rather than a claim of historical fact.

---

#### 1.5. Form Generation

The foundation of form comprises:

**Monumentality**  
Objects must possess visual weight and a sense of durability.

**Verticality**  
Columns, obelisks, elongated figures, and architectural axes emphasize scale.

**Symmetry**  
A central axis and mirrored or near-mirrored organization of elements are preferred.

**Modularity**  
The construction can be perceived as a system of repeating architectural and mechanical modules.

**Articulation**  
Biomechanical elements must appear as functional connections rather than random metallic ornaments.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional Protocol

Composition is built upon the principle of hierarchy:

**primary sacred or anthropomorphic object → technological construction → architectural environment → secondary details.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* pronounced central axis
* frontal or slightly perspective placement
* high visual stability
* prominent primary object
* controlled spatial depth
* architectural layers
* repeating geometric modules
* directional lighting
* limited secondary detailing

For artifacts, certificates, and institutional imagery, a frontal composition is preferred.

For scenes and illustrations, a pronounced perspective is permissible provided it preserves monumentality and constructive clarity.

Biomechanics must not disrupt the architectural composition.

---

#### 1.7. Color Protocol

**Primary Palette:**

* lapis lazuli blue
* gold
* white stone

**Secondary Palette:**

* bronze
* brass
* dark metal
* deep graphite

**Lighting Accents:**

* cool blue glow
* golden glow
* localized technological light sources

Color must not transform the image into a chaotic neon composition.

The primary visual mass is retained by lapis lazuli, stone, and metal.

---

#### 1.8. Lighting

Lighting is a constructive element.

Preferred:

* directional architectural lighting
* contrast of light and deep shadow
* golden reflections on metal
* cool technological glow
* illumination of sacred objects
* localized light sources within mechanical structures

Lighting must emphasize volume, material, and compositional hierarchy.

---

#### 1.9. Energy of the Style

I-1 must convey a sense of:

**power**  
**eternity**  
**sacred order**  
**technological complexity**  
**civilizational continuity**  
**human transformation**  
**eternal construction**

Primary emotional characteristic:

> **Sacred knowledge transformed into a technological system.**

---

#### 1.10. Human Figure

The human figure may act as:

* a bearer of technology
* a custodian of knowledge
* an operator of the system
* an architectural scale reference
* part of a biomechanical construction
* a symbolic character

The human figure must not automatically transform into a superhero.

Preferred:

* static posture
* dignity
* functionality
* monumentality
* controlled plasticity

Transhumanist elements are permissible if they possess constructive or conceptual significance.

---

#### 1.11. Technological Code

The technology of I-1 must appear as a **developed engineering system** rather than a collection of random sci-fi details.

Preferred:

* mechanical articulations
* energy nodes
* structural frames
* embedded interfaces
* technological conduits
* modular mechanisms
* mechanical prosthetics
* architectural integration of equipment

Modern technology must visually "grow into" ancient architecture.

The ideal result creates the sensation that the technological system was designed simultaneously with the temple.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the Style

I-1 **is not**:

* classical Egyptian art
* an archaeological reconstruction
* conventional cyberpunk
* steampunk
* fantasy with Egyptian decor
* horror
* gothic
* conventional sci-fi
* chaotic neon aesthetics
* a decorative collage of ancient symbols and modern mechanisms

Primary exclusion criterion:

> **If the ancient and technological elements exist side by side but do not form a unified structural system, the work does not correspond to I-1.**

---

#### 1.13. Principle of Visual Authenticity

Each essential element must fulfill at least one of three functions:

**constructive:**  
the element explains the device's assembly/structure;

**symbolic:**  
the element conveys meaning;

**compositional:**  
the element shapes the visual structure.

Elements existing solely for the sake of visual noise must be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible Degree of Stylization

I-1 permits a wide range from realistic depiction to conceptual art.

However, at any level of stylization, four invariants must be preserved:

**monumentality**  
**symmetry or controlled geometry**  
**material persuasiveness**  
**combination of sacred architecture and technological construction**

---

#### 1.15. Spheres of Application

**Institutional Documents**

* artifact passports
* certificates
* attestations
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing System**

* covers
* frontispieces
* dividers
* infographics
* monograph illustrations
* collector editions

**Visual Identity**

* emblems
* posters
* presentations
* digital interfaces
* exhibition materials

**Art Environment**

* conceptual imagery
* installations
* museum objects
* musical visual concepts
* collectible digital artifacts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance Control

An image is considered compliant with I-1 if it simultaneously features:

**1. A sacred architectural or symbolic layer.**

**2. A technological or biomechanical layer.**

**3. A unified material language of stone, metal, gold, and lapis lazuli.**

**4. A controlled geometric composition.**

**5. A sense of monumentality and durability.**

**6. A structural connection between the ancient and futuristic layers.**

If only Egyptian motifs are present, it is not I-1.

If only cyberpunk is present, it is not I-1.

If the ancient and futuristic elements are merely decoratively superimposed upon one another, it is likewise not I-1.

---

#### 1.17. Concise Formula for Generation

> **Monumental sacred architecture of Ancient Egypt integrated with functional biomechanics and a high-technology engineering system; lapis lazuli, gold, white stone, bronze, and dark metal; strict geometry, central compositional axis, deep architectural lighting, physically convincing materials, a sense of eternity, technological power, and civilizational continuity.**

---

#### 1.18. Style Directive

**I-1 does not depict the future alongside the past.**

**I-1 depicts a civilization in which the past has become the architectural foundation of the future.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the **IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum** architecture.

Each digital artifact (Digital Node) within our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authenticity system is structured, how to independently verify the cryptographic fingerprint of a file in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred alongside the artifact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and Functions of the Multipassport

The **Digital Node Passport** serves as the Single Source of Truth for each issued artifact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport, and the secure Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-tier scheme «Preview → Passport → Vault» -->

**Key Functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic fixation:** Guarantee that the master file you receive corresponds bit-for-bit with the author's original recorded at issuance.
- **Transparent provenance:** Public history of issuance, exemplar number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates, and ownership status.
- **Protection against counterfeiting and duplication:** Altering even a single pixel or metadata attribute within the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal-digital anchor:** The passport acts as the connecting link between the unique node ID and the type of transferred license.

---

#### 4.2. How to Verify Artifact Authenticity

Authentication requires no blind trust — it is based on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly within your browser**, without installing third-party software or utilizing proprietary services.

##### Step 1. Calculation of the Local Hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Upload the acquired master file from the Vault into the calculator field. It will calculate a **64-character digital fingerprint** directly within your browser — the file is not transmitted anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

##### Step 2. Cross-Checking with the Public Registry

1. Navigate to the passport link formatted as `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the line **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`**.
3. Compare the 64-character code calculated by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **100% Match:** The file is authentic, has undergone no modification or compression.
   - **Mismatch:** The file has been altered, compressed, or constitutes an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Registry Status Verification

Ensure that the public passport displays the current edition status (e.g., `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct license category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of Licensing Models (Digital Rights Framework)

All three models described below belong to the umbrella category of **Collector & Commercial Rights**, established in the archive manifest **RN-0044-GD-0001** (Section 8, Subsection 1). This category constitutes the general legal field of the series: it describes the full scope of rights that may ever be transferred, whereas each specific model defines the scope of rights received by the buyer.

Upon the acquisition or transfer of a digital node, one of the three licensing standards is fixed. The license type is embedded directly within the passport header and public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights levels -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License

- **Purpose:** Private collections, personal digital galleries, personal demonstration panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights for private exhibition on any personal devices and displays.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - Right to resell / transfer the exemplar with mandatory re-registration of ownership change in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** Commercial reproduction, utilization in advertising campaigns, and the resale of digital copies to third parties are strictly prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License

- **Purpose:** Media projects, corporate collections, utilization in architectural interior design, publications, and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - Right to commercial exhibition and integration of the artifact into composite media products.
  - Right to public reproducibility in printed or digital editions with mandatory indication of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** Authorial property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; the reassignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer

- **Purpose:** Complete buyout of the artifact with the transfer of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Complete transfer of commercial and property rights regarding the object to the buyer.
  - Closure of the edition buyout (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) within the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted source archive and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Vault Access Procedure (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** The rights transfer request is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique indicating the specific `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** The Archivist verifies compliance with conditions (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** Registry entries regarding the new owner are updated, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** The buyer receives a personal cryptographic key/link to download the original master file from the secure depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
