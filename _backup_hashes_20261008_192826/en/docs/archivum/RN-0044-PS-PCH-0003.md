#### 1. I-3 VISUAL STYLE PASSPORT

#### Series I

#### I-3. Veterans' Post-War Realism (Memory / Hope / Continuity)

#### 1.1. Style identification

**Code:** I-3
**Series:** I
**Name:** Veterans' Post-War Realism (Memory / Hope / Continuity)
**Type:** visual protocol / artistic and structural style

**Base formula:**

**1940s oil painting + poster graphics + sepia + Socialist Realist composition + documentary photography of destruction and rebuilding.**

The style is neither a literal reconstruction of a wartime poster nor a trite sentimental styling, but a visual language in its own right, in which tragedy, hope, respect and continuity exist as elements of a single act of witness.

---

#### 1.2. Conceptual core

The core principle of I-3:

> **The memory of destruction and the will to rebuild are not opposites, but two sides of a single act of witness.**

The past is perceived as a bearer of pain and loss, and at the same time as a source of strength.

The human figure represents not a lone hero but **a family, a community, a generation** carrying memory.

The environment joins both strata: ruins and shoots, fire and dawn.

Principal conceptual oppositions:

**destruction / rebuilding**
**mourning / hope**
**solitude / kinship**
**loss / continuity**
**past / future**
**war / peace**

---

#### 1.3. Material code

Principal materials and techniques:

**Oil painting**
Dense brushwork, the visible texture of canvas, warm depth of colour.

**1940s poster graphics**
Simplified silhouettes, clear composition, a legible message.

**Sepia and muted tones**
A muted palette as a sign of memory, not of a documentary present.

**Documentary photography**
An allusion to war reportage, archival photographs, family albums.

**Symbolic marks**
Doves, shoots, globes, diagrams of connection, silhouettes of family — as emblems of hope and rebuilding.

Materials must look physically convincing: real oil painting, not a digital filter. Real paper with typographic grain, not an imitation.

---

#### 1.4. Symbolic code

Permissible motifs:

* a family embracing amid the ruins
* a veteran in military uniform and out of it
* a child holding a glowing heart
* a woman bent over a table
* silhouettes of people against a backdrop of rebuilding
* shoots breaking through concrete
* doves of peace
* poster emblems: family, globe, shield, shoot, diagram of connection
* archival photographs and documents
* military and civilian clothing of the 1940s
* Ukrainian and international symbolism, in moderation

Symbols are used as part of the meaningful composition and respectful witness, not as random propaganda props.

---

#### 1.5. Form generation

Form is built upon:

**Painterly density**

Objects must possess mass, volume and a sense of the texture of oil paint.

**Human hierarchy**

The central figure (or group) — family, veteran, child — organizes the space around itself.

**Poster clarity**

Even a complex composition is legible at a glance. There is no visual noise.

**Documentary authenticity**

Clothing, poses and background correspond to the 1940s or to their continuation in the present day.

---

#### 1.6. Compositional protocol

Composition is built on the principle of poster hierarchy:

**principal human figure (or group) → symbolic elements → background environment (ruins, rebuilding) → accent lighting details.**

Preferred compositional characteristics:

* a pronounced central axis
* a stable composition
* soft depth of space
* directed light highlighting the face and hands
* a limited number of secondary details
* a background that works as context, not as a separate scene

---

#### 1.7. Colour protocol

**Primary palette:**

* sepia
* ochre, amber, warm gold
* muted grey-brown tones

**Secondary palette:**

* cold blue (for symbols of hope)
* dark green (for shoots, nature)
* black (for outline, depth)

**Light accents:**

* a warm glow at the centre of the composition
* glowing symbolic elements (globe, heart, shoot)
* soft morning light on the horizon

Colour must not become a garish palette. The principal visual mass remains with warm, muted tones.

---

#### 1.8. Light

Light is a bearer of meaning: it separates memory from the present.

Preferred:

* a warm glow emanating from the central figure or symbol
* soft light on faces, emphasizing weariness and dignity
* contrast between a lit present and a shadowed past
* a background glow on the horizon — a sign of dawn, of rebuilding

Light must emphasize the dignity of the figures, not their suffering.

---

#### 1.9. The energy of the style

I-3 must create a sense of:

**dignity**
**memory**
**hope**
**continuity**
**quiet strength**
**respect for loss**
**faith in rebuilding**

Principal emotional characteristic:

> **Memory become strength; loss become the foundation of the future.**

---

#### 1.10. The human figure

The human is the central element of the style.

The human may appear as:

* a veteran in uniform or out of it
* a mother, wife, daughter
* a child holding a symbol of hope
* a group of people united by shared memory
* a figure from an archival photograph

Preferred:

* restraint and dignity
* stillness and stability
* hands as a meaningful element (an embrace, a gesture of protection)
* an absence of pathos and posturing
* faces that carry memory but are not broken

The human figure must not automatically become a lone hero or a symbol of victimhood. It is a witness.

---

#### 1.11. Technological code

Technology in I-3 is presented **in moderation**:

* archival photographs, documents, letters
* symbolic digital diagrams (globe, network, connections)
* a glowing heart as a metaphor for continuity
* contemporary symbols in the poster idiom of the 1940s

Technology must not dominate the human figure. It is an **instrument for preserving memory**, not a theme in its own right.

---

#### 1.12. Boundaries of the style

I-3 **is not**:

* direct war propaganda
* a trite sentimental greeting card
* documentary reportage without artistic generalization
* historical reconstruction for its own sake
* a poster of enmity towards anyone
* kitsch on the theme of war

Principal exclusion criterion:

> **If human dignity is replaced by pathos, and memory by agitation, the work does not conform to I-3.**

---

#### 1.13. The principle of visual credibility

Every element must serve at least one of three functions:

**witness**
the element carries the memory of an event or an era;

**symbolic**
the element conveys hope, continuity, rebuilding;

**compositional**
the element organizes the space and holds the gaze.

Elements that exist for the sake of visual noise or cheap emotion are to be removed.

---

#### 1.14. Permissible degree of stylization

I-3 permits a range from photorealistic painting to poster graphics.

Whatever the level of stylization, four invariants are preserved:

**human dignity**
**a warm, muted lighting scenario**
**poster clarity of composition**
**a symbolic layer of hope and continuity**

---

#### 1.15. Fields of application

**Institutional documents**

* testimonials of memory
* certificates of authenticity
* title pages
* registration documents

**Publishing system**

* covers of documentary monographs
* illustrations for archival research
* collector's editions
* frontispieces

**Visual identity**

* commemorative series
* exhibition materials
* presentation campaigns
* digital interfaces

**Art environment**

* museum installations
* concept digital artefacts
* collectible digital works
* musical visual concepts

---

#### 1.16. Compliance control

An image is considered compliant with I-3 if it contains all of the following:

**1. A human figure (or group) as the meaningful centre of the composition.**

**2. A muted, warm colour scenario with symbolic accents.**

**3. Poster clarity of composition and legibility of message.**

**4. The background presence of context — destruction, rebuilding, memory.**

**5. A sense of dignity and continuity, rather than victimhood or agitation.**

If only war reportage appears, without artistic generalization, it is not I-3.

If only poster pathos appears, without human depth, it is not I-3.

---

#### 1.17. Short formula for generation

> **Oil painting in the spirit of the 1940s, sepia and muted warm tones, poster composition, a central figure of a family or a veteran, symbolic elements of hope (a shoot, a globe, a glowing heart), a background of destruction and rebuilding, human dignity without pathos, memory as strength, loss as the foundation of the future.**

---

#### 1.18. Style directive

**I-3 does not depict war for its own sake.**

**I-3 depicts memory become strength, and loss become the foundation of the future.**

#### 4. USER GUIDE: DIGITAL NODE PASSPORT & PROVENANCE REGISTRY

Welcome to the IMARCH / Chronicle OS / Verdictum architecture.

Every digital artefact (Digital Node) in our system is a registered system object. This guide explains how the authentication system works, how to verify a file's cryptographic fingerprint independently in three steps, and what digital rights are transferred with the artefact.

---

#### 4.1. Purpose and functions of the Multi-Passport

The **Digital Node Passport** is the single source of truth for every released artefact. It binds three layers into an indivisible chain: the public web preview, the public node passport and the secured Vault deposit.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Three-layer diagram "Preview → Passport → Vault" -->

**Key functions of the Passport:**

- **Cryptographic record:** a guarantee that the master file you receive matches the author's original, as recorded at the time of release, bit for bit.
- **Transparent provenance:** a public history of the release, the copy number within the edition (`Edition Index`), registration dates and ownership status.
- **Protection against forgery and duplication:** altering even a single pixel or metadata field in the file completely destroys the SHA-256 mathematical fingerprint.
- **Legal and digital anchor:** the Passport serves as the link between the node's unique ID and the type of licence being transferred.

---

#### 4.2. How to verify the authenticity of an artefact

Authentication requires no leap of faith — it rests on open mathematical standards. You can validate the received file **directly in your browser**, without installing third-party software and without using proprietary services.

#### Step 1. Calculate the local hash

Open our sovereign SHA-256 calculator:

→ **[VERDICTUM Hash Calculator](/ru/verdictum/hash-tool/)**

Load the master file you received from the Vault into the calculator field. It will compute the **64-character digital fingerprint** directly in your browser — the file is never sent anywhere; everything remains on your computer.

#### Step 2. Cross-check against the public registry

1. Follow the passport link in the format `imarch.ltd/nodes/NODE-ID/`.
2. Locate the **`SHA-256 Fingerprint`** line.
3. Compare the 64-character code computed by your browser with the code in the public passport.
   - **A 100% match:** the file is authentic and has not been modified or compressed.
   - **A mismatch:** the file has been altered or compressed, or is an unlicensed copy.

#### Step 3. Verify the registry status

Confirm that the public passport displays the current edition status (for example, `Edition 02/08 — Active / Transferred`) and the correct licence category.

---

#### 4.3. Overview of licensing models (Digital Rights Framework)

When a digital node is acquired or transferred, one of three licensing standards is recorded. The licence type is fixed directly in the passport header and the public registry.

<!-- INFOGRAPHIC SLOT: Triad of digital rights tiers -->

#### 4.3.1. Collector License (Personal Collecting Licence)

- **Purpose:** private collections, personal digital galleries, personal display panels.
- **Rights:**
  - Rights of private exhibition on any personal device or display.
  - Perpetual ownership of the provenance record in the public registry.
  - The right to resell or transfer the copy, with mandatory re-registration of the change of owner in the registry.
- **Restrictions:** commercial reproduction, use in advertising campaigns and the resale of digital copies to third parties are prohibited.

#### 4.3.2. Commercial & Publication License (Commercial and Publication Licence)

- **Purpose:** media projects, corporate collections, use in the design of architectural spaces, publications and branding.
- **Rights:**
  - The right to commercial exhibition and to integrate the artefact into composite media products.
  - The right to public reproduction in print or digital publications, with mandatory attribution of the `Node ID`.
- **Restrictions:** proprietary intellectual property rights (IP Core) remain with the Chronicle OS ecosystem; any assignment of sublicensing rights requires separate approval.

#### 4.3.3. Exclusive Transfer (Full assignment of exclusive rights)

- **Purpose:** outright purchase of the artefact with assignment of exclusive intellectual property rights.
- **Rights:**
  - Full transfer of commercial and proprietary rights in the object to the purchaser.
  - Closing of the edition purchase (`Edition Closed / Exclusive Sold Out`) in the public registry.
  - Transfer of the encrypted archive of source files and all associated working materials from the Vault.

---

#### 4.4. Procedure for obtaining Vault access (Fulfillment Workflow)

1. **Application:** a request for transfer of rights is submitted via the form in the Bonus Boutique, specifying the relevant `Node ID`.
2. **Verification:** the archivist checks that the conditions are met (entry threshold / payment) and initiates the minting/transfer procedure.
3. **Issuance:** the registry is updated with the new owner, and the final Passport is generated.
4. **Transfer:** the purchaser receives a personal cryptographic key/link for downloading the original master file from the closed depository (Vault) and an electronic version of the signed Passport.
